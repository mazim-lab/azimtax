HST for Renovators and Contractors in Durham Region: A Guide to Getting it Right

Published on March 29, 2026

As a contractor or renovator in booming Durham Region, you’re busy managing job sites, clients, and suppliers. The last thing you want to worry about is the complex world of Harmonized Sales Tax (HST). Yet, mishandling HST is one of the most common and costly mistakes a construction business can make. Understanding your obligations isn’t just about compliance; it’s about protecting your cash flow and profitability.

This guide breaks down the essentials of HST for contractors in Ajax, Pickering, Whitby, and across Durham, helping you build a financially sound business from the ground up.

1. The Mandatory Registration Rule

The first and most critical rule is knowing when you must register for an HST number. You are required to register for, collect, and remit HST if your gross, worldwide, taxable revenues are more than $30,000 in any single calendar quarter or in any four consecutive calendar quarters.

This is known as the "small supplier" threshold. For a growing contractor, it’s easy to cross this line. It's crucial to track your revenue closely. Once you exceed the $30,000 threshold, you are no longer a small supplier and must register with the CRA.

2. Collecting HST on Invoices: What to Charge For

Once registered, you must charge 13% HST on all your taxable supplies of goods and services. For a contractor, this includes:

  • Labour and Services: The fees you charge for your work are subject to HST.
  • Materials: Any materials you supply as part of the job (lumber, drywall, paint, etc.) are also subject to HST.
  • Subcontractor Fees: If you hire a subcontractor and bill the cost back to your client, you charge HST on the total amount billed.

Your invoices must clearly show your business name, the client's name, the date, your HST number, and a breakdown of the total amount charged plus the HST amount.

3. The Power of Input Tax Credits (ITCs)

Here’s where managing HST works in your favour. As an HST registrant, you can claim back the HST you pay on your legitimate business expenses. These are called Input Tax Credits (ITCs).

Think of all the HST you pay on a daily basis:

  • Materials and supplies from hardware stores.
  • Tool purchases and rentals.
  • Fuel for your work vehicle.
  • Safety equipment.
  • Professional services like accounting and legal fees.
  • Business-use portion of your cell phone and internet bills.

By claiming ITCs, you recover the HST paid on these expenses. The net amount you remit to the CRA is the total HST you collected from clients minus the total ITCs you are eligible to claim. Diligent expense tracking is key to maximizing your ITCs and improving your cash flow.

4. Common Pitfalls to Avoid

Many contractors run into trouble with the CRA by making a few common mistakes:

  • Forgetting to Register: Failing to register after crossing the $30,000 threshold can lead to backdated HST obligations, plus significant penalties and interest.
  • Using HST Collected as Cash Flow: The HST you collect from clients is not your money. It's held in trust for the government. Set it aside in a separate bank account so you are prepared when it's time to file your return.
  • Poor Record-Keeping: Without proper receipts and invoices for your expenses, you cannot claim ITCs. A simple bookkeeping system is essential for capturing every eligible credit.

Conclusion: Build a Strong Financial Foundation

Managing HST correctly is a fundamental part of running a professional and profitable renovation or construction business in Durham Region. It protects you from the CRA and ensures your business's financial health. If you're unsure about your obligations or need help setting up a system to track your collections and ITCs, professional guidance is a wise investment.
