September Bookkeeping Checklist for Durham Region Small Businesses: 7 Cleanups to Make Before Fall 2026

September is one of the best months for a bookkeeping reset. For many Durham Region small businesses, summer creates a quiet backlog: unreconciled accounts, missing receipts, payroll adjustments, and HST details that were easy to postpone during a busy season. Cleaning that up before fall gets fully underway can make the rest of 2026 much easier.

If you run a business in Oshawa, Whitby, Ajax, Pickering, or Clarington, the goal is not perfect books for their own sake. The goal is better visibility, fewer tax surprises, and cleaner records when you need to file, borrow, hire, or answer CRA questions. Here are seven bookkeeping fixes worth making before September gets hectic.

1. Reconcile every bank and credit card account through the latest month-end

Start with the basics. If your bank and credit card accounts are not reconciled, almost every report in your bookkeeping system becomes less trustworthy. September is a good time to catch up because you still have enough year left to use the numbers for planning. Review duplicate entries, uncleared payments, missing deposits, and transfers that were posted incorrectly. Small reconciliation errors tend to multiply when they sit too long.

2. Clear the uncategorized transactions before they pile up further

Most bookkeeping systems develop a messy corner where transactions sit without proper coding. That usually means personal charges mixed in with business expenses, vendor names that were imported badly, or items that need backup before they can be categorized properly. Leaving those entries unresolved makes month-end reporting weaker and HST filing harder. Spend time now assigning the right accounts and adding notes where a transaction may need extra context later.

3. Chase missing receipts while the details are still fresh

Receipt collection gets harder with time. By September, many owners can still remember what a July or August charge was for, but by tax season that memory is gone. Review fuel, meals, software, subscriptions, supplies, travel, and contractor costs. If you are using QuickBooks or another cloud system, attach the document right away. Good bookkeeping is not only about recording the number. It is about preserving the support behind the number.

4. Review payroll entries against what was actually paid and remitted

If your business has employees, payroll deserves its own bookkeeping review. Confirm that wages, source deductions, vacation pay, and employer costs were posted correctly in the books. Then compare those reports with the actual remittances sent to the CRA. September is especially useful for this if staffing changed over the summer or if hours fluctuated. Fixing payroll coding now is much easier than trying to untangle it at year-end or after a CRA notice arrives.

5. Clean up HST coding before your next return is due

Bookkeeping and HST problems usually travel together. If sales were coded inconsistently or expenses were posted without the right tax treatment, the next HST return can be wrong even if the total revenue number looks close. Review taxable sales, zero-rated items if any apply, and expenses where input tax credits may be limited or unclear. Mixed-use costs like vehicles, phones, and home office expenses are especially worth a second look.

6. Separate owner spending from real business expenses

Owner-managed businesses often drift into bad habits during busy months. Personal purchases land on business cards, shareholder draws are left in expense accounts, and reimbursements are recorded inconsistently. September is a smart time to clean that up. Clear separation helps with accurate financial statements, cleaner tax prep, and fewer awkward questions if the CRA reviews your records. It also gives you a more honest picture of what the business is actually spending to operate.

7. Turn your books into a fall planning tool

Once the cleanup is done, use the numbers. Review year-to-date revenue, margins, cash flow, and major expense trends before fall decisions start piling up. If sales are stronger than expected, you may need to increase tax set-asides. If cash is tighter than expected, this is the time to adjust spending, invoicing discipline, or owner withdrawals. Clean books are not just for compliance. They help you make better decisions while there is still time to act on them.

Why this September bookkeeping reset matters

When bookkeeping falls behind, business owners start relying on guesswork. That affects pricing, hiring, tax planning, and even basic confidence in the numbers. A short September cleanup can reduce stress for the rest of the year, make HST and payroll filing easier, and leave your year-end accountant with better records to work from. In many cases, a few hours of cleanup now saves days of confusion later.
