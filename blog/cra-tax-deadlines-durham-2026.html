Navigating CRA Deadlines: A 2026 Guide for Durham's Small Businesses

Published on February 28, 2026

For small business owners in Durham Region, juggling daily operations with financial obligations is a constant balancing act. Among the most critical responsibilities is meeting the Canada Revenue Agency's (CRA) deadlines. Missing one can lead to hefty penalties and unnecessary stress. With 2026 well underway, it's the perfect time to get organized.

At Azim Tax & Accounting, we want to ensure our local entrepreneurs from Ajax to Bowmanville are prepared. Here is a straightforward guide to the key CRA tax deadlines you can't afford to miss in 2026.

Key Deadline 1: Payroll Remittances

If you have employees, you are responsible for deducting Canada Pension Plan (CPP) contributions, Employment Insurance (EI) premiums, and income tax from their pay. These deductions, along with your share of CPP and EI, must be remitted to the CRA.

When it's due: Your remitter type determines your due date. Most small businesses are monthly remitters. This means your payroll deductions for a given month are due by the 15th of the following month. For example, deductions from February payroll are due by March 15th.

Pro Tip: Missing this deadline can result in a penalty of up to 20%. It's one of the most punitive deadlines, so always pay it on time.

Key Deadline 2: GST/HST Filing and Payment

For most small businesses, Goods and Services Tax (GST) or Harmonized Sales Tax (HST) is collected throughout the year. The frequency of filing—monthly, quarterly, or annually—depends on your business revenue.

When it's due (for annual filers):

  • Filing Deadline: If you are a sole proprietor, your GST/HST return is due by June 15, 2026. For corporations, it's typically three months after your fiscal year-end.
  • Payment Deadline: This is the crucial part. For all annual filers, the payment deadline is April 30, 2026, regardless of your filing deadline.

Key Deadline 3: Personal & Small Business Income Tax (T1)

If you are a sole proprietor or in a partnership, you report your business income on your personal T1 income tax return.

When it's due:

  • Filing Deadline: As a self-employed individual, you (and your spouse or common-law partner) have until June 15, 2026, to file your T1 return.
  • Payment Deadline: The CRA requires any balance owing to be paid by April 30, 2026. Even though you have until June to file, interest will start accumulating on any unpaid amount after April 30th.

Key Deadline 4: Corporate Income Tax (T2)

For incorporated businesses in Canada, the rules are different. Your corporate tax return has its own set of deadlines based on your corporation's fiscal year-end.

When it's due:

  • Filing Deadline: Your T2 return must be filed within six months of your corporation's fiscal year-end. For a business with a December 31st year-end, the deadline would be June 30, 2026.
  • Payment Deadline: Generally, corporate taxes are due within two months of the fiscal year-end. For a December 31st year-end, payment is due by the end of February. Some smaller Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) may have a three-month payment window.

Stay Organized, Stay Compliant

Marking these dates in your calendar is the first step. The next is ensuring your bookkeeping is up-to-date so you can file and pay accurately. Proactive financial management not only saves you from CRA penalties but also provides a clear picture of your business's health.
