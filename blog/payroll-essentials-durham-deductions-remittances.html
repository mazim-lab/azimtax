Payroll Essentials for Durham Businesses: Navigating Deductions and Remittances

Published on March 14, 2026

For small business owners in Durham Region, managing payroll goes beyond simply paying employees. It involves a complex web of deductions, remittances, and compliance with Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) regulations. Getting it right is crucial to avoid penalties, maintain employee satisfaction, and ensure your business operates smoothly. At Azim Tax & Accounting, we help businesses in Whitby, Oshawa, Ajax, and across the region navigate these complexities. Let's break down the essentials of payroll deductions and remittances.

Understanding Core Payroll Deductions

Every time you pay an employee, you're responsible for withholding certain amounts from their gross earnings. These are known as "source deductions" and typically include:

1. Canada Pension Plan (CPP) Contributions

Both employees and employers contribute to the Canada Pension Plan. These contributions are mandatory for most workers in Canada, providing contributors with retirement, disability, and survivor benefits. The amount deducted depends on the employee's earnings and the annual CPP maximums set by the CRA. As an employer, you are responsible for remitting both your portion and the employee's portion to the CRA.

2. Employment Insurance (EI) Premiums

Employment Insurance provides temporary financial assistance to unemployed Canadians who have lost their jobs through no fault of their own. Like CPP, both employees and employers contribute to EI. Employers pay 1.4 times the employee's premium rate. These funds are vital for supporting workers during periods of unemployment, illness, or parental leave, and proper remittance is a cornerstone of your payroll obligations.

3. Income Tax

As an employer, you must withhold federal and provincial (Ontario, in this case) income tax from your employees' pay. The amount to withhold is based on the employee's income, their tax claims on Form TD1 (Personal Tax Credits Return), and the CRA's payroll deduction tables. Accurate income tax withholding ensures that employees meet their tax obligations throughout the year, preventing large tax bills at year-end.

Calculating and Remitting Source Deductions

Calculating source deductions can be intricate, especially with varying provincial rates and annual changes to maximum contributions. The CRA provides detailed Payroll Deductions Online Calculator and manual deduction tables to assist employers. However, for many small businesses in Durham, utilizing payroll software or a professional service is often the most efficient and error-free approach.

Once deductions are calculated and withheld, they must be remitted to the CRA. Your remittance due date depends on your average monthly withholding amount:

  • New employers or those withholding less than $1,000 per month: Remit by the 15th day of the month following the month in which you paid your employees.
  • Regular remitters (average monthly withholding between $1,000 and $24,999.99): Remit by the 15th day of the month following the month in which you paid your employees.
  • Accelerated remitters (average monthly withholding of $25,000 or more): Remit twice a month, by the 25th day of the month for amounts paid from the 1st to the 15th, and by the 10th day of the following month for amounts paid from the 16th to the end of the month.

Failing to remit on time can result in significant penalties and interest charges from the CRA, impacting your business's financial health.

Other Important Payroll Considerations

Record Keeping

Meticulous record keeping is non-negotiable. You must keep records of:

  • Employee names, addresses, and SINs.
  • Dates of employment.
  • Amounts paid (gross and net).
  • All deductions withheld.
  • Hours worked.

These records are essential for preparing T4 slips at year-end and for any potential CRA audits. Keep them organized and accessible for at least six years.

T4 Slips

By the end of February each year, you must prepare and distribute T4 slips (Statement of Remuneration Paid) to your employees. These slips report their employment income and the deductions withheld during the previous calendar year. A copy must also be filed with the CRA. Accurate T4 reporting is critical for both your employees' tax filing and your own compliance.

Workplace Safety and Insurance Board (WSIB) in Ontario

While not a CRA deduction, most employers in Ontario are required to register with the Workplace Safety and Insurance Board (WSIB) and pay premiums. WSIB provides no-fault insurance for workplace injuries and illnesses. Your premiums are calculated based on your industry, payroll, and safety record. Timely reporting of payroll and remittance of premiums is another vital compliance area for Durham businesses.
