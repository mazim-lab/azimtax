Family Tax Credits 2026: Maximizing Returns for Durham Region Families

Published on February 27, 2026

Raising a family in Durham Region—whether you're in Oshawa, Whitby, Ajax, or Pickering—comes with significant expenses. From childcare and extracurriculars to the rising cost of groceries, every dollar counts. Fortunately, the Canadian tax system offers several credits and deductions designed specifically to help families offset these costs.

As we enter the core of the 2026 tax filing season (for the 2025 tax year), ensuring you claim every eligible credit is crucial. At Azim Tax & Accounting, we specialize in helping local families optimize their returns. Here is a breakdown of the key family tax credits you shouldn't miss this year.

1. Canada Child Benefit (CCB) Updates

The Canada Child Benefit (CCB) is a tax-free monthly payment made to eligible families to help with the cost of raising children under 18 years of age. While you don't "claim" this on your return in the traditional sense, you must file your tax return on time every year to continue receiving payments, even if you have no income to report.

For the payment period starting July 2026, the benefit amounts are indexed to inflation. Filing your 2025 return accurately ensures your payments are calculated correctly based on your adjusted family net income (AFNI).

2. Child Care Expense Deduction

Childcare is one of the largest expenses for working parents. You can claim eligible child care expenses paid to:

  • Daycare centers and nursery schools
  • Caregivers (nannies) providing care in your home
  • Day camps and day sports schools (primary goal must be childcare)
  • Boarding schools and overnight camps

Key Tip: Generally, the spouse with the lower net income must claim this deduction. Make sure you have receipts that include the SIN of the individual provider or the business number of the daycare center.

3. Eligible Medical Expenses

Families often overlook out-of-pocket medical expenses. You can claim a wide range of costs that aren't fully covered by your insurance plan, including:

  • Prescription medications
  • Dental and orthodontic work
  • Glasses and contact lenses
  • Tutoring for children with learning disabilities (with a practitioner's certification)
  • Travel expenses for medical treatment if you travel more than 40km (or 80km) for services not available locally

You can claim expenses for any 12-month period ending in 2025 that haven't been claimed previously. It's usually beneficial for the lower-income spouse to claim these, but we can run the numbers to see which scenario yields the best refund.

4. The Canada Dental Benefit & Pharmacare

As the government rolls out new phases of national dental and pharmacare coverage, eligibility criteria often link back to your filed tax return. Ensuring your family income is reported accurately is essential to qualify for these evolving programs if you don't have private insurance.

5. Tuition Transfer for Post-Secondary Students

If you have a child attending Durham College, Ontario Tech University, or another post-secondary institution, they will receive a T2202 form for their tuition. Students must use their tuition credits to reduce their own tax payable first, but they can transfer up to $5,000 of the unused amount to a parent or grandparent.

This can result in significant tax savings for parents. Ensure your child signs the transfer section of their tax certificate so we can apply it to your return.

6. Adoption Expenses

If you adopted a child in 2025, you can claim eligible adoption expenses (agency fees, legal fees, travel) for the adoption period. This is a non-refundable tax credit that can help offset the high costs associated with expanding your family.

Don't Leave Money on the Table

Tax laws change frequently, and missing a deduction is easy when you're juggling work and family life. At Azim Tax & Accounting, we review your specific situation to ensure you aren't missing out on the credits you deserve.
