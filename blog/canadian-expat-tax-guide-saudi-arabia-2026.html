Canadian Expat Tax Guide for Saudi Arabia: What to Know Before You Move

Moving to Saudi Arabia can change your day-to-day tax life fast, but it does not automatically end your Canadian tax questions. What matters most is your residency status, the ties you keep to Canada, and the income or property that still connects you to the Canadian tax system.

For Canadians heading to Saudi Arabia or anywhere else in the Middle East, the goal is not just to file something at year-end. The goal is to understand what changes when you leave, what stays on your Canadian return, and what records you need so you do not create avoidable problems later.

1. Residency matters more than your visa

A work visa, contract, or foreign address does not by itself decide whether you are still a Canadian tax resident. The CRA looks at your residential ties: where your home is, whether your spouse or dependants remain in Canada, whether you keep a Canadian mailing address, and how much of your life is still anchored here.

If you are trying to determine whether you have become a non-resident, do not guess. That decision affects what income you report, what forms you file, and how certain Canadian-source items are taxed.

2. Your departure return is a checkpoint

The year you leave Canada usually requires a departure return. That return reports your income up to the date you left and marks the point where your Canadian residency changed, if it did change. It is also where certain assets may need extra attention, including investments and other property that can trigger deemed disposition rules.

This is one of the places where people get tripped up. They leave Canada, keep earning income abroad, and only later realize the departure date should have been documented more carefully. Clean records make the filing much easier.

3. Saudi salary is not always the whole story

Saudi Arabia does not tax employment income the same way Canada does, which is one reason many Canadians assume the Canadian side disappears too. It does not work that way. If you remain a Canadian resident, your worldwide income is still relevant. If you become a non-resident, certain Canadian income sources can still be taxed or withheld.

That can include rental income, dividends, pensions, RRSP withdrawals, business income, and some investment income. If you have more than one income stream, each one needs to be looked at separately instead of lumped together.

4. Canadian property needs separate planning

If you keep a house, condo, or rental property in Canada while living in Saudi Arabia, your tax filing can become more complicated quickly. Rental property creates reporting obligations. A future sale can trigger non-resident rules, withholding requirements, and possible clearance-certificate work.

Even if the property seems passive, it is still part of your tax picture. Keep track of purchase price, mortgage interest, repairs, property taxes, insurance, and the dates you were present or absent. Those details matter when the CRA asks questions.

5. Keep the paperwork that proves your story

Cross-border tax work goes much smoother when your records are organized. Save the following before and after your move:

  • Your departure date from Canada and travel records
  • Proof of where you lived before and after leaving
  • Employment contracts and pay slips from Saudi Arabia
  • Statements for Canadian bank, RRSP, TFSA, and investment accounts
  • Rental income and expense records for any Canadian property
  • Notices, slips, and CRA correspondence in one folder

If you can show the timeline clearly, tax filing becomes much simpler. If you cannot, the return becomes a guessing game, and that is usually where mistakes start.

6. Plan for the day you return

Many Canadians go abroad for a few years and then come back. That return matters too. If you re-establish Canadian residency later, the CRA may want to know when that happened and what changed. A return move can affect your reporting obligations, your investment setup, and how you handle income earned around the transition.

Think of the move as a chapter, not a permanent exit. The cleaner your records are while you are abroad, the easier it is to reset when you come home.
