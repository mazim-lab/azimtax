Spring HST Checklist for Durham Region Small Businesses (2026)

If your business collects HST, spring is a good time to clean up the numbers before the next filing deadline sneaks up on you. A short review now can help you avoid missed input tax credits, surprise balances owing, and last-minute stress. This checklist is designed for Durham Region small businesses that want to stay organized and CRA-compliant without turning tax season into a crisis.

Why spring is the right time to review HST

By April, many businesses have already closed one quarter or are halfway through their annual reporting cycle. That makes spring the perfect point to check whether your books are actually matching what happened in the business. If sales, expenses, or staffing changed over the winter, your HST records may need a reset.

It is also a smart time to identify problems before they become expensive. A missing receipt, an incorrect coding rule in QuickBooks, or a sale recorded without HST can all create avoidable adjustments later. A quick spring review is easier than fixing an entire year of bookkeeping at once.

1. Confirm that every sale was charged correctly

Start with revenue. Review your invoices, point-of-sale reports, and bank deposits to make sure HST was charged where required. This matters most if you sell a mix of taxable and exempt products or services, or if you occasionally invoice customers outside Ontario.

  • Check that HST was added to taxable sales.
  • Confirm that exempt items were not taxed by mistake.
  • Review discounts and refunds so they are reflected correctly.
  • Make sure deposits match your sales reports after tax is removed.

If your business uses multiple staff members to invoice clients, this is one of the easiest places for errors to creep in. One inconsistent invoice template can create a chain reaction in the books.

2. Review input tax credits before you file

One of the biggest HST mistakes small businesses make is failing to claim input tax credits properly. If you paid HST on eligible business expenses, you may be able to recover that tax through your return. But only if the expense was truly business-related and supported by a valid receipt.

Take a fresh look at common categories:

  • Office supplies and software subscriptions
  • Advertising and marketing costs
  • Professional fees such as bookkeeping or legal support
  • Vehicle expenses, if tracked separately
  • Phone, internet, and utilities for business use

Personal purchases mixed into the same account are where things get messy. If you paid for both business and personal items on the same card, you need a clean allocation. Guessing is not a strategy the CRA likes.

3. Watch for mixed-use and home office expenses

Many Durham Region owners work from home at least part-time. That can be perfectly fine, but mixed-use expenses need careful handling. Only the business portion of shared costs should be claimed. If 30% of your internet bill supports the business, then only that portion should be included in your HST claim.

The same idea applies to vehicle expenses, meals, cell phones, and rent in some cases. Keep your method consistent and document how you arrived at the percentage. If you cannot explain the calculation later, it will be difficult to defend.

4. Reconcile your books to your bank account

Before filing, reconcile every HST-relevant account to the bank and credit card statements. This is where you catch duplicated entries, missing expenses, and deposits that were recorded in the wrong period. If your books are not reconciled, your HST return is basically built on sand.

A proper reconciliation should answer three questions:

  1. Did all taxable sales get recorded?
  2. Did all eligible HST-bearing expenses get entered?
  3. Do the totals in the books match what actually cleared the bank?

Even if you only do bookkeeping once a quarter, a monthly reconciliation saves time. It is much easier to fix small issues every few weeks than to clean up a year-end pile of transactions.

5. Check your filing frequency and due date

Not every business files HST on the same schedule. Some file monthly, some quarterly, and some annually. Your filing frequency affects when the return is due, so it is worth confirming before the deadline arrives. The CRA generally bases the due date on your assigned reporting period, not on when you happen to get around to the work.

If your business is growing quickly, your filing pattern may also change over time. That can happen after a registration update or if the CRA reviews your account. If you are unsure, check your CRA My Business Account or ask your bookkeeper to verify the current frequency.

6. Build a simple filing habit

The best HST system is boring. That is a compliment. Boring systems work.

Here is a simple routine that helps most small businesses:

  • Set aside HST collected in a separate bank account.
  • Save receipts as they come in, not weeks later.
  • Reconcile monthly, even if you file quarterly.
  • Review sales tax treatment before sending invoices.
  • Keep a calendar reminder at least two weeks before filing.

Once this becomes routine, HST stops being a scramble and starts being a normal part of operations.
