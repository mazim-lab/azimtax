Don't Risk Your CCB: Why Filing Your Taxes on Time is Crucial

For many Durham Region families, the Canada Child Benefit is a vital part of the monthly budget. Here’s why meeting the tax deadline is non-negotiable to protect it.

As tax season gets into full swing across Durham Region, most people are focused on a single question: "Will I get a refund or do I owe?" But for families with children under 18, the stakes are much higher. Filing your annual income tax return is directly linked to one of the most important government benefits: the Canada Child Benefit (CCB).

At Azim Tax & Accounting, we often consult with clients in Whitby, Oshawa, and Ajax who don't realize that delaying their tax filing can lead to a sudden and stressful interruption of their CCB payments. Let's break down why filing on time is so critical for your family's finances.

How Tax Returns and the CCB Are Connected

The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) administers the CCB, a tax-free monthly payment designed to help with the cost of raising children. The amount you receive is not arbitrary; it's calculated based on your Adjusted Family Net Income (AFNI) from the previous year's tax return.

Here’s the timeline:

  • You file your 2025 tax return by the April 30, 2026 deadline.
  • The CRA processes this return and determines your AFNI.
  • Based on this 2025 income level, the CRA calculates your CCB payments for the next benefit year, which runs from July 2026 to June 2027.

If you (and your spouse or common-law partner) don't file your returns, the CRA has no information to work with. They cannot verify your income, so they cannot calculate your benefit. As a result, your payments will stop.

What Happens If You File Late?

Let's say you miss the April 30 deadline and don't file until August. The CRA will eventually process your return, and if you're eligible, they will retroactively pay any CCB amounts you were owed.

However, this means you will have missed your July and August payments, and potentially September's as well, depending on processing times. This creates a significant cash flow gap that can disrupt your household budget, making it difficult to pay for essentials like groceries, childcare, or extracurricular activities.

The simple rule is: No tax return, no benefit payments.

It's Not Just the CCB

Filing your taxes on time is also crucial for several other federal and provincial benefits that are often paid alongside the CCB, including:

  • The GST/HST credit
  • The Ontario Trillium Benefit (OTB)
  • Provincial child disability benefits

A single late tax filing can freeze multiple streams of income your family may depend on.

What If Your Marital Status Changed?

Life happens. If you separated, divorced, or married in 2025, it is vital to update your marital status with the CRA and file your taxes correctly. Your CCB entitlement is based on your current family situation and your previous year's family income.

Filing your return accurately ensures the CRA has the right information to calculate the correct benefit amount. Failing to report a change could lead to overpayments that you'll have to pay back, or underpayments that you'll have to wait for.

Protect Your Family's Benefits

Your tax return is more than just a summary of the past year—it's the key to unlocking the government benefits your family is entitled to for the coming year. Don't leave it to chance or wait until the last minute.
