Mid-Year Tax Planning for Durham Region Small Businesses: What to Review Before Summer Gets Busy

By mid-June, many Durham Region business owners are deep into day-to-day work and not thinking much about tax planning. That is exactly why this is a useful time to stop and review the numbers. A short mid-year check can help you avoid cash flow surprises, catch filing issues early, and make smarter decisions before the busy fall stretch begins.

Whether you run a service business in Whitby, a trades company in Oshawa, or a retail operation in Ajax or Pickering, summer is a good point to review what the first half of 2026 actually looked like. Here are the tax and bookkeeping areas worth checking now.

1. Compare your actual profit to what you expected

Many tax problems start with outdated assumptions. If revenue is higher than expected, your tax bill may also be higher than expected. If profit is lower, you may need to adjust spending plans, owner draws, or instalment expectations.

Run a year-to-date profit and loss report and compare it with the same period last year. Look for major swings in revenue, gross margin, subcontractor costs, advertising, vehicle expenses, and payroll. Mid-year is early enough to react, which is much better than discovering the gap after year-end.

2. Review whether you should be setting aside more for income tax

Small business owners often focus on sales and forget that higher income usually means a larger tax obligation later. If the business is performing well, make sure cash is being set aside instead of treated as fully available spending money.

A separate tax savings account can help. Even a simple monthly transfer based on current profitability is better than waiting until filing season and hoping the bank balance works itself out.

3. Clean up HST before the next filing period arrives

HST gets harder when it is left alone for too long. Mid-year is a good time to confirm that sales are being taxed correctly, receipts are attached to expenses, and input tax credits are being claimed only when the support is there.

Three quick checks go a long way:

  • Make sure bank and credit card accounts are reconciled.
  • Review uncategorized transactions in your bookkeeping software.
  • Confirm that owner spending has not been mixed into business expenses.

Fixing those issues now is much easier than doing a full cleanup right before a filing deadline.

4. Check payroll remittances and source deduction records

If you have employees, summer is a smart time to make sure payroll records are clean. Remittance problems can become expensive because penalties and interest stack up quickly when deductions are late or underpaid.

Review your payroll reports and confirm that gross pay, CPP, EI, and income tax withholdings match what was actually remitted. If you use a payroll platform, do not assume everything posted correctly without checking. One missed remittance can create months of follow-up work.

5. Revisit how you are paying yourself

For incorporated owners, mid-year is also a sensible time to review salary versus dividends. The right mix depends on cash flow, personal tax planning, CPP goals, and how consistent business income looks for the rest of the year.

You do not need to solve the entire compensation strategy in June, but you do want enough time to make adjustments before December. Waiting until year-end reduces your flexibility.

6. Chase missing paperwork while it is still easy to find

June is much closer to January than next March will be. If you are missing receipts, loan statements, contractor invoices, or mileage support, now is the time to collect them. The longer bookkeeping stays incomplete, the more likely details get lost and the harder it becomes to defend deductions later.

A simple document routine helps: keep digital copies in one folder, upload receipts as they happen, and avoid leaving support inside a truck console or kitchen drawer until tax season.

7. Use summer to prevent a stressful fall

Good tax planning is rarely about a dramatic last-minute move. More often, it is about getting organized early enough that the fall months feel manageable. When bookkeeping is current, tax cash is set aside, and payroll or HST issues are cleaned up mid-year, the rest of the year becomes much easier to navigate.

A short review now can save time and money later

Mid-year is one of the best times to pause, review the books, and make practical corrections while there is still room to act. That is especially true for Durham Region small businesses with busy summer schedules, seasonal work, or growing payroll.
