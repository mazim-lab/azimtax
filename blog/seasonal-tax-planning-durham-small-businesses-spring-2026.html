Spring Tax Planning for Durham Region Small Businesses: What to Review Before Q2

Spring is a good time to slow down, clean up the books, and make sure tax season does not turn into next quarter’s headache. For Durham Region small businesses, a few smart reviews now can improve cash flow, reduce CRA surprises, and make your next filing cycle much easier.

1. Reconcile your books before the next busy stretch

Bookkeeping gets messy fast when receipts pile up, bank feeds are left unreconciled, or personal and business spending mix together. Before Q2 gets moving, review the basics:

  • Match bank and credit card transactions to your records.
  • Follow up on missing receipts, duplicate entries, and uncategorized expenses.
  • Check whether owner draws, shareholder loans, or personal transfers were recorded correctly.

Clean books make every other tax decision easier. They also help you spot profit trends early, which matters if sales are up and so are your remittance obligations.

2. Review HST before it becomes a catch-up project

If you collect HST, spring is a good time to confirm that your filing cadence matches your actual sales activity. Many businesses get busy, then discover their HST records are a month or two behind. That can lead to rushed filings, missed input tax credits, or a payment that is larger than expected.

Ask yourself:

  1. Are all taxable sales being charged HST correctly?
  2. Are you claiming input tax credits on eligible purchases?
  3. Do your sales reports agree with your bookkeeping software?

If your revenue is growing quickly, also keep an eye on the small supplier threshold. Once you cross it, registration and filing responsibilities can change.

3. Plan for payroll costs, not just wages

Payroll is more than paying staff. It includes source deductions, employer CPP, EI, vacation pay, and timing. Spring is often when owners realize their payroll cash flow is tighter than expected because deductions were treated like ordinary operating expenses.

A simple improvement is to separate payroll cash the same day wages are run. If your business has seasonal swings, use a separate savings account or cash reserve so remittances are not an emergency later in the month.

4. Update your tax estimate if revenue changed

If your sales are stronger this year, your tax bill may be stronger too. That matters for sole proprietors, incorporated businesses, and owners taking dividends or salary. A spring check-in can prevent underpayment surprises near year-end.

Look at year-to-date profit, not just revenue. Higher sales do not always mean higher cash if expenses, wages, and inventory also increased. Still, if profit is trending up, it may be time to increase instalment planning or set aside more for income tax.

5. Tighten up expense tracking now

Spring is usually when the first quarter’s records tell the truth. If expense tracking has been inconsistent, fix the process before the year runs away from you. Good habits here save time at tax filing and improve your numbers all year long.

  • Use one business account for business spending.
  • Store receipts digitally as soon as you buy something.
  • Create simple categories for common costs like fuel, software, meals, and advertising.

The goal is not fancy accounting. The goal is records you can trust when it is time to file, borrow, budget, or make a hiring decision.

6. Don’t ignore notices and deadlines

Many small business tax problems start with a small CRA notice that sits unopened. Spring is a good reminder to check your CRA mail, online accounts, and any notices from your accountant. If something is unclear, deal with it early. It is usually easier to correct a problem in April or May than after several filing periods have passed.

When a spring tax review makes sense

A quick seasonal review is especially useful if you:

  • Had a strong Q1 and expect more growth.
  • Added employees or contractors recently.
  • Collect HST and want cleaner filings.
  • Have fallen behind on bookkeeping.
  • Want to avoid year-end stress and catch issues early.
