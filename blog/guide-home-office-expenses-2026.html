The Ultimate Guide to Claiming Home Office Expenses in 2026

Published on February 27, 2026

For many entrepreneurs and small business owners in Durham Region, the line between home and office has blurred. Working from a home office offers flexibility and savings, but it also presents a significant opportunity to reduce your taxable income. The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) allows you to deduct a portion of your home's operating costs, but the rules can be complex.

As you prepare your 2025 tax return, understanding these rules is key to maximizing your claim without raising red flags. Here's what you need to know about claiming home office expenses.

Eligibility: Can You Claim These Expenses?

To be eligible to deduct home office expenses, you must meet one of two conditions:

  1. Your home is your principal place of business. This means you spend more than 50% of your working time in your home office.
  2. The space is used exclusively for earning business income, and you use it on a regular and continuous basis for meeting clients, customers, or patients.

If you're a sole proprietor or in a partnership, you'll claim these expenses on the Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities. Note that the simplified flat-rate method introduced during the pandemic is no longer available for the 2024 tax year and beyond.

How to Calculate the Business-Use Portion

You can't deduct 100% of your home expenses. You must calculate the portion of your home that is used for business. The most common and CRA-accepted method is to use square footage.

Formula: (Square footage of your office / Total square footage of your home) x 100 = Business-use percentage.

For example, if your office is 200 square feet and your home is 2,000 square feet, your business-use percentage is 10%. This is the percentage of your eligible home expenses that you can deduct.

What Expenses Can You Deduct?

Once you have your business-use percentage, you can apply it to a range of household expenses. These include:

  • Utilities: Heat, electricity, and water.
  • Home Internet Access Fees: You can deduct the business portion of your monthly internet bill.
  • Maintenance and Minor Repairs: This includes costs like cleaning supplies, light bulbs, or repairing a broken window.
  • Rent: If you rent your home, you can deduct the business-use portion of your annual rent.
  • Property Taxes and Home Insurance: If you own your home, you can deduct the business-use portion.

What You Generally Cannot Deduct

There are some expenses you cannot claim as part of your home office deduction:

  • Mortgage interest (though it may be claimable elsewhere).
  • Capital Cost Allowance (CCA) or depreciation on the business-use part of your home. Claiming this can affect the principal residence exemption when you sell your home. We strongly advise against this for most homeowners.
    • - Expenses for which you have been reimbursed.

Important Limitations

Your home office expense deduction cannot create a business loss. You can only deduct these expenses up to the amount of net income your business earns before the deduction. If you can't use all of your home office expenses in one year, you can carry the excess forward to the next year, as long as you are still earning income from the same business.

Stay Organized and Keep Records

The CRA requires you to keep detailed records and supporting documents for all claimed expenses. This means holding onto utility bills, rent receipts, property tax statements, and invoices for any repairs or maintenance. Organization is your best defense in the event of an audit.
