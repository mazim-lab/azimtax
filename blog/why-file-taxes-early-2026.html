Why Filing Your 2026 Tax Return Early Pays Off

Procrastination costs more than you think. Here is why getting ahead of the April 30th deadline is the smartest financial move you can make this spring.

It’s late February in Durham Region. The snow is (hopefully) melting, days are getting longer, and somewhere in the back of your mind, a little voice is whispering: "I still haven't done my taxes."

For many Canadians, tax season is a source of anxiety, leading to a natural tendency to push it off until the last possible weekend in April. But at Azim Tax & Accounting, we see firsthand the difference between clients who file early and those who scramble at the deadline. Spoiler alert: the early filers are usually happier (and sometimes richer).

Here are five compelling reasons to stop procrastinating and get your 2025 tax return filed now.

1. Get Your Refund Faster

This is the most obvious benefit, but it bears repeating. If the CRA owes you money, filing early means that money lands in your bank account sooner. Why let the government hold onto your hard-earned cash interest-free for another two months?

Did you know? If you file electronically with direct deposit set up, you can often receive your refund in as little as two weeks. That’s extra cash flow for spring renovations, a vacation, or paying down holiday debt.

2. Avoid the "Late Filing Penalty" Trap

A common misconception is that if you can't afford to pay your tax bill, you should wait to file. This is dangerous advice.

The CRA imposes a late-filing penalty of 5% of your balance owing, plus 1% for every full month your return is late (up to 12 months). Even if you can't pay a cent right now, filing on time avoids this penalty entirely. You'll only face interest on the balance owing, which is much cheaper than the penalty + interest combo.

3. More Time to Plan Payment

Filing early doesn't mean paying early. If you file in March and discover you owe $2,000, you don't have to pay it immediately. The payment deadline is typically April 30th.

By filing now, you give yourself a two-month "head start" to budget and save for that bill. Finding out you owe money on April 29th leaves you with zero wiggle room and a lot of stress.

4. Prevent Benefit Interruptions

Many government benefits—like the Canada Child Benefit (CCB) and the GST/HST Credit—are calculated based on your tax return. If you file late, the CRA may not have your information in time to calculate your payments for the new benefit year (which starts in July).

We've seen Durham families miss crucial monthly payments simply because they filed a few weeks late. Don't let paperwork disrupt your household budget.

5. Beat the Rush (and Get Better Service)

Tax accountants in Oshawa, Whitby, and across the GTA are swamped in the final two weeks of April. Appointment slots disappear, response times slow down, and stress levels spike.

By booking your appointment with Azim Tax in February or March, you ensure you get our undivided attention. We have more time to review your file thoroughly, dig for overlooked deductions, and answer your questions without the pressure of a looming deadline.

Ready to Check It Off Your List?

Tax season doesn't have to be a headache. Whether you have a simple T4 slip or a complex portfolio of investments and rental properties, getting it done early is the best way to secure your peace of mind.
