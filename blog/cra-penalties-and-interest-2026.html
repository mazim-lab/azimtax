Don't Be Late: Understanding CRA Penalties and Interest on Your 2025 Taxes

Published on March 3, 2026

For small business owners across Durham Region, from Pickering to Port Perry, meeting Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) deadlines is a critical part of the annual financial cycle. While many focus on getting the numbers right, filing and paying on time is just as important. The financial consequences of missing these deadlines can be significant, turning a manageable tax bill into a much larger financial burden through penalties and high interest charges.

1. The Late-Filing Penalty: An Expensive Mistake

The CRA imposes a penalty the moment you file your tax return after the deadline. If you owe taxes, this penalty is calculated as:

  • 5% of your 2025 balance owing, plus
  • 1% of your balance owing for each full month your return is late, to a maximum of 12 months.

For example, if you owe $5,000 and file three months late, the initial penalty would be $250 (5%) plus an additional $150 (1% x 3 months), for a total of $400. This is a substantial penalty for simply missing a date.

If you were also charged a late-filing penalty in any of the three preceding years, the penalties can double to 10% of your balance owing, plus 2% per month for up to 20 months.

2. Prescribed Interest: A Compounding Problem

On top of the late-filing penalty, the CRA charges interest on any unpaid tax balance. This interest starts accruing the day after the payment deadline and compounds daily. The rate is what's known as the "prescribed interest rate," which is set by the CRA quarterly and can change throughout the year.

For Q1 and Q2 of 2026, the prescribed interest rate on overdue taxes is a staggering 10%. This is significantly higher than most commercial loans or lines of credit. Unlike the late-filing penalty, there is no maximum limit to the amount of interest the CRA can charge. It will continue to accumulate until the entire balance—including penalties and previously accrued interest—is paid in full.

3. Key Deadlines for 2025 Tax Year (Filing in 2026)

To avoid these charges, marking your calendar is essential:

  • April 30, 2026: The deadline for most Canadians to file their personal income tax return. It's also the deadline to pay any tax you owe.
  • June 15, 2026: The deadline for self-employed individuals and their spouses or common-law partners to file their returns. Crucially, even if you have until June to file, your payment is still due on April 30, 2026. Interest will start accruing on any outstanding balance from May 1st.

4. What If You Can't Pay? File Anyway!

This is perhaps the most important piece of advice for any business owner in Durham Region facing a cash flow crunch. Even if you cannot pay your full tax bill by the deadline, you should always file your return on time.

By filing on time, you avoid the hefty late-filing penalty. You will still be charged interest on the amount you owe, but you will have prevented the immediate 5% (or 10%) penalty from being applied. This gives you time to arrange payment with the CRA, potentially through a payment plan, without the extra burden of the failure-to-file penalty.

Conclusion

Meeting your tax obligations is more than just about accurate bookkeeping; it's about timely compliance. The CRA's penalty and interest structure is designed to discourage tardiness, and the costs can quickly spiral. By understanding the deadlines and the consequences of being late, you can protect your business's bottom line.
