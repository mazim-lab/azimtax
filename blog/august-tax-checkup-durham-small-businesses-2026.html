August Tax Checkup for Durham Region Small Businesses: 7 Smart Moves Before Fall 2026

August is a useful checkpoint for Durham Region small business owners. Summer is far enough along that you can see what the year is actually doing, but there is still enough time to fix tax and bookkeeping problems before fall gets busy.

For businesses in Oshawa, Whitby, Ajax, Pickering, and Clarington, the goal is not to wait for year-end and hope the numbers work out. It is to review the tax items that tend to create surprises, penalties, or cash flow pressure when they get ignored too long. Here are seven practical tax moves worth making now.

1. Check whether you should be setting aside more for income tax

If your business had a stronger first half than expected, August is a good time to revisit your tax set-aside habit. Many owner-managed businesses look profitable on paper but forget that corporate tax or personal tax on draws is still coming later. If you are self-employed, the same issue shows up when strong summer revenue creates a bigger balance owing than expected.

Review year-to-date net income and compare it with what you have actually set aside. Even a rough estimate now is better than waiting until filing season. A separate tax savings account can make this much easier to manage.

2. Review your HST before the next filing date sneaks up

HST problems often build quietly. A few miscoded sales, missing receipts, or personal expenses mixed into business purchases can distort your return without being obvious day to day. August is a good month to spot-check HST coding while the transactions are still fresh.

Focus on three areas: taxable sales, input tax credits, and expenses with mixed personal and business use. If you have been growing quickly, also confirm that the filing frequency and reporting method you are using still make sense for the business as it operates now.

3. Make sure payroll remittances match your records

For employers, payroll is one of the fastest ways to create avoidable CRA trouble. Source deductions are not optional cash flow tools, and even small errors can repeat across multiple pay runs. Before the fall hiring or back-to-school shift begins, compare payroll reports against what was actually remitted.

Check CPP, EI, and income tax deductions, as well as vacation pay treatment and any taxable benefits. If you use software, do not assume every setup detail is correct forever. Changes in wages, staff status, or pay timing can create errors that only show up months later.

4. Clean up expenses while you still remember what they were for

One of the easiest wins in August is expense cleanup. Reconcile credit cards and bank accounts, attach missing receipts, and review uncategorized transactions. This helps from both a bookkeeping and tax perspective because deductible expenses are easier to defend when the records are current and complete.

Vehicle costs, meals, home office expenses, software subscriptions, and contractor payments are all worth reviewing. If something looks questionable now, it will not look better in March when you barely remember the transaction.

5. Revisit owner pay: salary, dividends, or draws

Many small business owners drift into a payment pattern without revisiting whether it still fits the year. If you are incorporated, August can be a good time to review whether you have been taking too much or too little out of the company, and whether the balance between salary and dividends deserves another look.

If you are unincorporated, check whether owner withdrawals are putting pressure on HST, payroll, or income tax cash reserves. Profit does not equal free cash, and that distinction matters more in the second half of the year.

6. Get ready early for any CRA notices or instalments

Late summer is when many businesses realize they have ignored a CRA letter, missed an instalment expectation, or postponed a response that should have been handled sooner. If you have any open CRA mail, review it now. The earlier you deal with notices, the more options you usually have.

This also applies to tax instalments. If the CRA expects instalments for your business or for you personally, August is the right time to confirm the schedule and make sure cash is available. Penalties and interest rarely feel urgent until they start compounding.

7. Use August to make year-end easier, not harder

The best August tax tip is simple: do not treat year-end as a rescue mission. If your books are up to date by late summer, you can make smarter decisions on spending, compensation, HST, and tax planning before the year closes. If they are months behind, you are operating with a blindfold on.

A short review now can save hours later. It can also reduce accounting fees, lower the chance of missed deductions, and give you a clearer picture of what the business can actually afford heading into fall.

What Durham Region business owners should do next

If you own a small business in Durham Region, use August to tighten the basics: current books, clean HST records, matched payroll remittances, and a realistic tax reserve. These are not glamorous tasks, but they are the ones that keep small problems from becoming expensive ones.
