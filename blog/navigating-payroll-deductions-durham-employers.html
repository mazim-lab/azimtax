Navigating Payroll Deductions: A Guide for Durham Region Employers

Published on March 26, 2026

For any business owner in Durham Region with employees, payroll is more than just issuing paychecks. It involves a complex web of responsibilities, particularly when it comes to source deductions. Understanding and correctly remitting payroll deductions to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) is not just good practice; it's a legal obligation that, if mishandled, can lead to significant penalties and interest. At Azim Tax & Accounting, we work with numerous businesses across Whitby, Oshawa, Ajax, and Pickering, helping them streamline their payroll processes. Let's demystify payroll deductions and equip you with the knowledge to stay compliant.

What Are Payroll Deductions?

Payroll deductions, also known as source deductions, are amounts that employers must subtract from their employees' gross pay and remit to the CRA on their behalf. These deductions typically include:

  • Canada Pension Plan (CPP) contributions: Both employers and employees contribute to CPP, which provides contributors with retirement, disability, and survivor benefits.
  • Employment Insurance (EI) premiums: Employees pay EI premiums, which fund benefits for temporary unemployment, sickness, maternity/paternity leave, and more. Employers also pay a portion of EI premiums.
  • Income Tax: The amount of federal and provincial (Ontario) income tax deducted depends on the employee's total earnings and their TD1 forms (Personal Tax Credits Return).

It's crucial for Durham Region employers to understand that these funds are held in trust for the CRA. Failing to deduct or remit them correctly and on time can have serious consequences for your business.

Employer Responsibilities: Beyond Just Deducting

Your duties as an employer extend beyond simply taking money off a paycheque. Key responsibilities include:

1. Calculating Deductions Accurately

The CRA provides detailed guides and online calculators to help you determine the correct amounts for CPP, EI, and income tax. These rates and thresholds change annually, so it's vital to use the most current information. Factors such as an employee's gross earnings, their claimed tax credits (from their TD1 forms), and the annual maximums for CPP and EI all influence the calculation. For many small businesses in Durham Region, using reliable payroll software or seeking professional payroll advice can prevent costly errors.

2. Remitting to the CRA on Time

Once deductions are made, they must be remitted to the CRA. Your remittance due date depends on your average monthly withholding amount (AMWA) from two calendar years ago. Most small businesses in Durham will be regular remitters, meaning payments are due by the 15th day of the month following the month you paid your employees. For new employers, you are generally considered a regular remitter until the CRA notifies you otherwise. Missing these deadlines incurs penalties, so timely remittances are paramount.

3. Issuing T4 Slips Annually

By the last day of February each year, you must provide your employees with a T4 Statement of Remuneration Paid slip for the previous calendar year. You must also submit a T4 information return (T4 Summary and all T4 slips) to the CRA. These slips summarize the employment income and all source deductions made. Accuracy here is critical, as it directly impacts your employees' personal tax filings.

4. Maintaining Meticulous Records

Keep comprehensive records of all payroll-related activities, including:

  • Employee information (name, SIN, address, hire date)
  • Hours worked and gross earnings
  • All deductions made (CPP, EI, income tax, other benefits)
  • Remittance dates and amounts to the CRA
  • Copies of TD1 forms and T4 slips

These records are essential for internal audits, CRA reviews, and resolving any discrepancies that may arise.

Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

Even diligent Durham Region employers can stumble. Here are common issues and how to prevent them:

  • Incorrect Calculations: Rely on up-to-date CRA resources or quality payroll software.
  • Late Remittances: Set up automated reminders or use a professional payroll service to ensure timely payments.
  • Missing TD1 Forms: Ensure all new employees complete both federal and provincial TD1 forms before their first payday.
  • Co-mingling Funds: As with expenses, keep payroll remittances separate from operating funds.
  • Ignoring Changes: Stay informed about annual updates to CPP, EI, and income tax rates and maximums.
