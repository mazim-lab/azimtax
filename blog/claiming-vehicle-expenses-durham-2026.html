A Small Business Owner's Guide to Claiming Vehicle Expenses in Durham Region

Published on March 2, 2026

For many small business owners in Durham Region, from contractors in Oshawa to consultants driving between Ajax and Whitby, a vehicle is an essential business tool. The costs associated with using that vehicle for business can add up to a significant tax deduction, but the rules can be tricky. The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) has specific requirements for claiming these expenses, and getting it right is key to maximizing your claim and avoiding issues during an audit.

1. The Golden Rule: Business Use vs. Personal Use

The most critical concept to understand is that you can only deduct the portion of vehicle expenses related to earning business income. You cannot claim expenses for driving from home to your place of work, or for any other personal travel. This is why tracking your mileage is non-negotiable.

  • Business Kilometres: Travel to meet clients, pick up supplies, attend conferences, or make bank deposits.
  • Personal Kilometres: Commuting, running personal errands, or any other travel not directly related to your business operations.

2. The Logbook: Your Most Important Piece of Evidence

The CRA requires you to keep a detailed logbook to support your vehicle expense claims. Without it, your claim could be denied. Your logbook must record:

  • Total Kilometres: The odometer reading at the start and end of the fiscal year.
  • Business Trips: For each business trip, you must log the date, destination, purpose of the trip, and the number of kilometres driven.

You don't necessarily need a paper book; there are many great smartphone apps that can make tracking this information seamless. The key is to be diligent and consistent. A full logbook for the entire year is best, but the CRA may accept a three-month sample logbook if it is representative of your typical business use for the year.

3. What Expenses Can You Claim?

Once you've determined the percentage of your vehicle use that is for business (e.g., 15,000 business km / 25,000 total km = 60% business use), you can claim that percentage of your total vehicle costs. Eligible expenses include:

  • Fuel and oil
  • Insurance
  • License and registration fees
  • Maintenance and repairs
  • Leasing costs (with limits)
  • Interest on a car loan (with limits)
  • Capital Cost Allowance (CCA), which is the depreciation on the vehicle

Keep every single receipt for these expenses. A dedicated folder or a digital scanning app can help you stay organized.

4. Special Considerations: Leased vs. Financed Vehicles

The rules differ slightly depending on whether you lease or own your vehicle.

  • Leased Vehicles: You can deduct the business portion of your lease payments, up to a CRA-specified limit. For 2026, this limit is typically around $950 + HST per month, but always check the current rates.
  • Financed/Owned Vehicles: You cannot deduct the principal portion of your loan payments. Instead, you claim Capital Cost Allowance (CCA), which allows you to deduct a percentage of the vehicle's capital cost each year. There are also limits on the capital cost of passenger vehicles that you can claim.

Conclusion

Properly tracking and claiming vehicle expenses is a powerful way to lower your taxable income. The key is meticulous record-keeping. By maintaining a detailed logbook and keeping all your receipts, you can ensure you get the full deduction you're entitled to.
