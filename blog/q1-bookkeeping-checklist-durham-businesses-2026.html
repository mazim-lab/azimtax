Finalizing Your Q1 Books: A Checklist for Durham Small Businesses

Published on March 28, 2026

As the first quarter of 2026 comes to a close, it's the perfect time for small business owners in Durham Region to get their financial house in order. A clean, accurate set of Q1 books not only provides a clear picture of your business's health but also makes tax time significantly less stressful. For many corporations with a December 31st year-end, the deadline to pay any taxes owed was March 31st, making Q1 reporting critical.

Whether you're in Oshawa, Whitby, or Ajax, staying on top of your bookkeeping from the start sets the tone for the entire year. Use this checklist to confidently close out your first quarter.

1. Gather and Organize All Financial Documents

The first step is to collect all relevant paperwork from January, February, and March. Don't let receipts pile up in a shoebox.

  • Bank and Credit Card Statements: Download PDF statements for all business accounts. These are crucial for reconciliation.
  • Receipts and Invoices: Digitize all paper receipts for business expenses. Ensure every sale is backed by an invoice.
  • Payroll Reports: If you have employees, gather your payroll summaries for the quarter, including records of remittances paid to the CRA.

2. Reconcile Your Bank Accounts

Bank reconciliation is the process of matching the transactions in your accounting software (like QuickBooks) with your bank statements. This is a non-negotiable monthly task, but a quarterly review is essential to catch any discrepancies.

  • Go line by line through your statements and ensure every transaction is correctly categorized in your books.
  • Investigate any unidentified transactions immediately. Was it a business expense or a personal draw?
  • Confirm that all payments received from clients have been recorded and deposited.

3. Review Accounts Receivable and Payable

Your quarterly-end gives you a great snapshot of who owes you money and who you owe.

Accounts Receivable (A/R):

  • Run an A/R aging report to see which invoices are outstanding.
  • Follow up promptly with clients on any overdue payments. Consistent follow-up improves cash flow.
  • Make sure all payments received in Q1 have been correctly applied to their respective invoices.

Accounts Payable (A/P):

  • Review all bills and supplier invoices to ensure they are recorded and scheduled for payment.
  • Take note of payment terms to avoid late fees and maintain good relationships with your vendors.

4. Categorize Expenses and Review Spending

With all your transactions in one place, take the time to categorize them properly. This is vital for claiming the right deductions at tax time.

  • Are your vehicle expenses, marketing costs, office supplies, and meals correctly separated?
  • Review your spending patterns. A quarterly review can reveal where your money is going and highlight potential areas for savings in Q2.

5. Prepare for HST/GST Remittance

If you are an HST/GST registrant, the end of the quarter often aligns with a filing deadline. Accurate books are essential for an accurate filing.

  • Calculate the total HST/GST collected on sales.
  • Tally up the Input Tax Credits (ITCs) from your business expenses.
  • The difference is what you owe to the CRA (or what you will get back as a refund). Ensure this amount is set aside and ready for remittance.

Conclusion: Start Q2 on the Right Foot

Closing your Q1 books isn't just about looking back; it's about setting your business up for future success. A tidy set of books gives you the clarity to make smart decisions, the confidence to manage your cash flow, and a head start on a stress-free tax season.
