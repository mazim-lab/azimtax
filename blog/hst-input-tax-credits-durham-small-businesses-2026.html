HST Input Tax Credits for Durham Region Small Businesses: 7 Records CRA May Expect

For many HST-registered businesses, input tax credits reduce the net tax sent to the Canada Revenue Agency. The calculation may look simple—HST collected minus eligible HST paid—but the records behind each claim matter. Missing invoices, personal expenses, and inconsistent bookkeeping can turn a routine HST return into an expensive cleanup.

Whether you run a trades business in Oshawa, a consulting practice in Whitby, or a retail operation in Ajax or Pickering, a repeatable documentation process can help you claim eligible credits with confidence. Here are seven areas Durham Region small business owners should review before their next HST filing.

1. Keep invoices that identify the supplier

A credit card statement proves that money changed hands, but it may not show what you bought, how much HST was charged, or who supplied it. Keep the original invoice or receipt. Depending on the purchase amount, CRA documentary requirements can include the supplier's name, invoice date, total paid, HST amount or rate, and GST/HST registration number. Larger purchases generally require more detail, including the buyer's name and a description of the goods or services.

2. Confirm the expense supports commercial activities

An input tax credit is generally tied to property or services acquired for use in your commercial activities. Personal purchases do not become eligible simply because they were paid from a business account. For each transaction, the bookkeeping record should make the business purpose clear. If your business makes both taxable supplies and exempt supplies, the available credit may need to be allocated rather than claimed in full.

3. Document mixed business and personal use

Vehicles, mobile phones, internet service, and home-office costs often have both business and personal elements. Record the method used to calculate the business portion and apply it consistently. A vehicle log, itemized phone bill, or reasonable workspace calculation can support the percentage claimed. Do not automatically claim all HST shown on a mixed-use bill. The income tax deduction and the HST input tax credit also follow different rules, so one percentage should not be copied blindly to every calculation.

4. Treat meals and entertainment carefully

Business meals are a common source of errors. Even when the expense has a legitimate business purpose, the recoverable HST is generally restricted in line with the income tax limitation, subject to specific exceptions. Keep the detailed receipt and note who attended and why the meeting was business-related. Separating meals from ordinary travel or office costs in your bookkeeping makes the limitation easier to apply and review.

5. Record capital purchases separately

Computers, tools, equipment, furniture, and vehicles should not be buried in a general supplies account. Identify capital assets separately and retain purchase agreements, invoices, financing documents, and proof of payment. The HST treatment can depend on the asset, its commercial-use percentage, and how the business is structured. Passenger vehicles can be subject to additional limits. A clear asset record helps your bookkeeper evaluate the credit correctly and supports future income tax and disposition reporting.

6. Reconcile HST accounts before filing

Before submitting a return, compare the HST collected on sales with your sales reports and general ledger. Then review HST paid on purchases for duplicate entries, missing receipts, tax coded on zero-rated or exempt items, and expenses posted to the wrong reporting period. Your HST payable or refund should reconcile to the balance sheet after accounting for prior returns and payments. This check is especially important when several people enter transactions or when feeds import data automatically.

7. Store records so they can be retrieved

CRA generally requires relevant business records and supporting documents to be retained for six years from the end of the last tax year they relate to, although exceptions can apply. A box of faded thermal receipts is not a reliable system. Scan documents promptly, use consistent file names, and link receipts to transactions in your accounting software. Keep secure backups and make sure records remain readable if you change software or bookkeepers.

Build a cleaner HST filing process

A strong HST process happens throughout the reporting period, not on filing day. Collect invoices as purchases occur, separate business and personal spending, review unusual transactions, and reconcile the tax accounts monthly. If documentation is incomplete, resolve it before claiming the credit rather than assuming the bank statement is enough.
