Beyond the Basics: 3 Advanced HST Tips for Durham Businesses

Published on March 1, 2026

Once you've mastered the fundamentals of collecting and remitting HST, you can start leveraging more advanced strategies to improve your cash flow and simplify your accounting. For service-based businesses and retailers in Whitby, Oshawa, and across Durham Region, understanding these nuances can make a significant difference. Here are three advanced HST tips that go beyond the basics.

1. The Quick Method: Simplifying Your HST Remittance

For many small businesses, the traditional method of tracking every dollar of HST collected and every Input Tax Credit (ITC) paid can be time-consuming. The CRA offers an alternative: the Quick Method of Accounting. This method simplifies HST filing by allowing you to remit a lower percentage of the HST you collect.

  • How it Works: Instead of tracking all your ITCs, you remit a portion of your sales revenue using a prescribed lower rate. The rate varies depending on your business type (e.g., service-based, retail). You also get a 1% credit on the first $30,000 of eligible sales.
  • Who is Eligible? Your business's annual worldwide taxable supplies (including GST/HST) must not exceed $400,000. There are some exceptions, so it's crucial to check the CRA's eligibility criteria.
  • The Trade-Off: The main advantage is simplified bookkeeping. The potential downside is that you cannot claim ITCs on most of your operating expenses (you can still claim them on capital asset purchases). For businesses with low expenses, the Quick Method can often result in paying less HST.

2. Managing HST on Deposits and Pre-payments

If your business accepts deposits or pre-payments from clients, it's important to know when to account for the HST. Do you charge it when the deposit is paid or when the service is rendered? The general rule is based on when the payment is applied.

  • Deposits as Security: If a deposit is held strictly as security and is refunded to the client upon completion of a contract or return of property, it is not considered a payment for a supply, and therefore, HST is not applicable on the deposit amount.
  • Deposits as Partial Payment: However, if the deposit is to be applied as a partial payment for the goods or services you are providing, then HST is due on the amount of the deposit at the time you receive it. You must report this HST on the return for the period in which the deposit was collected.

Properly categorizing deposits is crucial for accurate HST reporting and avoiding issues during a CRA audit. Always clarify the purpose of the deposit in your client agreements.

3. Recovering HST from Bad Debts

Unfortunately, not all invoices get paid. When a client fails to pay you for goods or services you've provided, it becomes a "bad debt." The good news is that you don't have to be out of pocket for the HST you've already remitted on that sale. You can recover it.

If you have already accounted for the full HST on a sale and remitted it to the CRA, but have since written off the corresponding receivable as a bad debt in your books, you can claim an adjustment on your HST return. This allows you to recover the HST amount you previously paid to the CRA on the unpaid invoice.

To make this claim, you must have written the debt off in your books of record. Should the client pay you later, you would then be required to add the HST amount back to a future return.

Conclusion

Mastering these advanced HST concepts—the Quick Method, the treatment of deposits, and bad debt recovery—can significantly enhance your financial management. It streamlines your processes and ensures you're not paying more tax than necessary.
