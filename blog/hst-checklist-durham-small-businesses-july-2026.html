HST Tips for Durham Region Small Businesses: 7 Checks to Make Before Your Next Filing

HST problems usually do not start with one big mistake. They build from small coding errors, missing receipts, forgotten adjustments, and rushed filing reviews. For small business owners in Oshawa, Whitby, Ajax, Pickering, and Clarington, a short pre-filing checklist can prevent avoidable interest, penalties, and cleanup work later.

If your next GST/HST return is coming up, these seven checks are worth making before you file.

1. Confirm that all sales are coded properly

Start by reviewing revenue for the filing period. Make sure taxable sales were actually coded as taxable, zero-rated items were not mixed in by accident, and exempt revenue was treated correctly. Service businesses often run into trouble when invoices are created outside the bookkeeping system and entered later in batches. If the sales numbers in your books do not tie back to what you invoiced and deposited, your HST return may already be off before you even look at expenses.

2. Review input tax credits before claiming everything

Many business owners assume every expense with HST on it can be claimed in full. That is not always true. If an expense is partly personal, relates to exempt activity, or was entered with the wrong tax code, the input tax credit may need to be reduced or removed. Meals, vehicle costs, home office expenses, and mixed-use phone or internet bills are common problem areas. A quick review now is much easier than explaining weak support later.

3. Check that receipts and supplier invoices are complete

Good HST claims need good documentation. Before filing, look for missing receipts, incomplete invoices, or transactions that were imported from the bank feed without proper backup. The bookkeeping entry alone is not the full audit trail. If you are claiming HST on a cost, you should be able to show who charged it, when it was charged, and how much tax was included. Digital storage is fine, but the support needs to exist.

4. Reconcile the HST account to the return period

Your HST payable account should not just be a mystery number sitting on the balance sheet. Review the account activity and make sure it reflects the filing period you are about to report. Old journal entries, duplicated adjustments, and prior-period corrections can distort the balance and create confusion about what is actually owing. If the HST account cannot be explained clearly, stop and sort it out before transmitting the return.

5. Watch for owner-paid and reimbursed expenses

In owner-managed businesses, it is common for a shareholder or sole proprietor to pay for business items personally and record them later. That is fine if the bookkeeping is handled properly, but these transactions are often missed or coded inconsistently. Before filing, review reimbursable expenses and shareholder loan postings so you do not miss legitimate HST claims or accidentally double-count them.

6. Make sure your filing frequency still fits the business

Some Durham Region businesses start with annual HST filing and later realize quarterly filing would improve cash-flow discipline. Others file more frequently than necessary and create extra admin pressure for no real benefit. Your assigned filing frequency depends on your revenue profile, but it is still worth reviewing whether your current workflow matches the pace of the business. Filing is easier when your bookkeeping routine and remittance schedule actually make sense together.

7. Set aside the cash before you file

One practical HST habit matters more than people admit: separating the cash mentally and operationally before the due date arrives. If collected HST sits in the same account as operating cash with no monitoring, it tends to get spent. Even businesses with solid sales can feel squeezed when remittance time arrives. A simple monthly estimate or transfer habit can make the filing process far less stressful and reduce the temptation to delay payment.

A cleaner HST process starts before the deadline

Filing an HST return is easier when the review happens before the due date, not the night before. Clean sales coding, support for input tax credits, reconciled balances, and a clear view of cash owed can turn HST from a recurring scramble into a manageable routine.
