Canadian Expat Tax Guide: What to Do When You Move to the UAE

Moving to Dubai, Abu Dhabi, or anywhere in the UAE can be exciting, but your Canadian tax situation does not disappear when your plane lands. A clean exit matters.

For many Canadians, the UAE is a strong destination because of career opportunities, family ties, and the absence of personal income tax. But from a CRA perspective, leaving Canada is not just a change of address. It can trigger a departure return, residency changes, and reporting obligations that are easy to miss if you rush the move.

If you are planning a move from Ontario to the Middle East, here is the practical checklist I would want you to work through before you go.

1. Figure out your Canadian tax residency first

The first question is not where you are living. It is whether you are still a Canadian tax resident. The CRA looks at residential ties such as a home in Canada, a spouse or dependants remaining here, provincial health coverage, bank accounts, and even where your car is kept.

If you cut most significant ties and establish a life in the UAE, you may become a non-resident for tax purposes. If you keep major ties in Canada, you may remain a factual resident even while working abroad. This is where many people get tripped up, because immigration status and tax residency are not the same thing.

2. Watch for the departure return

When you leave Canada and become a non-resident, the CRA generally expects a final return for the year you depart. That return usually includes your departure date and may require you to report certain assets at fair market value.

This can create departure tax, sometimes called a deemed disposition, on some investments and property. Your RRSPs are usually not treated the same way, but taxable brokerage holdings, shares, and some other assets can be affected. The exact treatment depends on what you own and when you leave.

3. Decide what to do with your Canadian home

If you keep a house or condo in Canada and rent it out after moving, you may need to deal with rental income, NR6 filings, and non-resident withholding rules. If you sell before leaving, there may be a principal residence exemption to consider.

Timing matters. A sale before departure can be much simpler than trying to sort out rental compliance from overseas. If you plan to keep the property, make sure the paperwork is handled before the first rent cheque comes in.

4. Tell the right institutions before you leave

This part is boring, but essential. Update your bank, investment firm, employer, insurance provider, and the CRA with your new address and non-resident status if applicable. If you are leaving a Canadian payroll job, make sure your final slips and T4 records are correct.

You should also review your TFSA and FHSA status. A TFSA can remain open after you leave Canada, but contribution room does not continue to build while you are a non-resident. FHSA rules are even more restrictive, so it is worth checking before departure.

5. Know what happens to your investments

Some Canadians keep trading in their brokerage accounts after moving abroad without realizing that investment income can still be taxable in Canada, depending on the source. Dividend income, interest, and capital gains may also be subject to withholding tax or treaty rules.

For UAE-bound clients, I always recommend a quick review of the portfolio before departure. It is much easier to simplify your holdings in Canada than to untangle them after you have already settled overseas.

6. Don’t ignore provincial and family details

Ontario residency can still matter for health coverage, drivers’ licences, and your overall ties. If your spouse or children remain in Canada, that can weigh heavily in the CRA’s residency analysis.

In other words, one person moving to the UAE may become a non-resident, while another person in a similar job may remain resident because their family and home stayed in Canada. The facts matter more than the flight path.

7. Get the filing sequence right

The cleanest approach is usually:

  • confirm your departure date
  • review your residential ties
  • prepare your final Canadian return
  • deal with any rental or investment reporting
  • set up any non-resident filings that continue after you leave

That sequence saves headaches later, especially if you are juggling a new job, visa paperwork, and international banking at the same time.

Moving abroad should not turn into a tax mess

The UAE can be a great move for Canadian professionals, entrepreneurs, and families. But if you do not plan the tax side, you can end up with double filings, missed withholding, or an unnecessary CRA problem months later.

If you are heading to Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Riyadh, Doha, or anywhere else in the region, get your Canadian exit sorted before you go. A short planning session now can save a lot of cleanup later.
