Small Business Payroll Guide: Meeting the 2026 T4 Deadline

Published on February 22, 2026

For small business owners in Durham Region and across Canada, February is crunch time. While many focus on personal tax returns due in April, employers have a much earlier and equally critical deadline: T4 filing.

This year, the standard February 28 deadline falls on a Saturday. That means the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) has extended the filing deadline to the next business day: Monday, March 2, 2026. Whether you have one employee or fifty, missing this date can lead to immediate penalties.

Who Needs to File a T4?

As an employer, you must file a T4 slip for every employee who received remuneration during the 2025 calendar year if:

  • You paid them more than $500; OR
  • You deducted CPP/QPP contributions, EI premiums, PPIP premiums, or income tax from their pay.

This includes salaries, wages, tips, bonuses, vacation pay, and taxable benefits (like company car allowances or health premiums paid by you). Even if an employee no longer works for you, if you paid them in 2025, you owe them—and the CRA—a T4 slip.

The Cost of Being Late

The CRA takes payroll deadlines seriously. Unlike personal tax returns where penalties are often based on the balance owing, T4 late-filing penalties are based on the number of slips filed late.

Penalties start the day after the deadline. For small businesses filing 1 to 5 slips, the penalty is $10 per day, with a minimum penalty of $100 and a maximum of $1,000. For larger businesses, the daily fines increase rapidly. And remember, this penalty is just for filing late—it doesn't include interest on any unremitted source deductions you might still owe.

Common Payroll Mistakes to Avoid

Rushing to meet the deadline often leads to errors. Here are a few we commonly see in Durham Region businesses:

  1. Incorrect SINs: Verify every employee's Social Insurance Number (SIN). A T4 with a wrong or missing SIN can be rejected.
  2. Missing Taxable Benefits: Did you give employees gift cards, a transit pass, or pay for their personal life insurance? These are often taxable benefits that must be included in Box 14 and specific other codes.
  3. Box 45 (Dental Benefits): Don't forget the new reporting requirements for dental coverage (Box 45). You must indicate if an employee and their family members were eligible for dental insurance coverage on December 31st of the tax year.

How to File

Most small businesses now file electronically. You can use:

  • Web Forms: Great for filing 1 to 100 slips directly on the CRA website.
  • Internet File Transfer (XML): Used by payroll software like QuickBooks Online, Wagepoint, or Rise.
  • My Business Account: If you are registered, you can file directly through your portal.

Note for 2026: If you file more than 5 information returns (T4s), you must file electronically. Paper filing is only allowed for very small employers (5 slips or fewer), but electronic is faster and safer for everyone.
