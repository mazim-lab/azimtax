HST Essentials for Durham Region's Small Businesses

Published on February 20, 2026

For many small business owners in Durham Region, from Pickering to Clarington, managing the Harmonized Sales Tax (HST) can feel like a complex puzzle. When do you need to register? How do you collect it properly? What can you claim back? At Azim Tax & Accounting, we believe that with a little guidance, HST can become a straightforward part of your business operations. This guide will walk you through the essentials.

Understanding the HST Basics

In Ontario, the HST is a 13% tax applied to most goods and services. It combines the 5% federal Goods and Services Tax (GST) and the 8% Provincial Sales Tax (PST). As a business owner, you act as a collector of this tax on behalf of the government.

When Do You Need to Register for an HST Number?

This is the most common question we receive. You are required to register for, collect, and remit HST if your business's total worldwide taxable revenues (before expenses) are more than $30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over the last four consecutive calendar quarters. This is known as the "small supplier" threshold.

  • Exceeding the Threshold: Once you exceed this $30,000 limit, you are no longer considered a small supplier and must register for an HST account.
  • Voluntary Registration: Even if you are below the threshold, you can choose to register voluntarily. Why would you do this? The main advantage is the ability to claim Input Tax Credits (ITCs).

The Power of Input Tax Credits (ITCs)

Input Tax Credits are your key to making HST more manageable. ITCs allow you to recover the HST you pay on your legitimate business expenses and purchases. Think of it as a refund for the HST you've paid out.

  • What Can You Claim? You can claim ITCs on a wide range of business costs, including office supplies, equipment purchases, rent, utilities, and professional fees like legal or accounting services.
  • The Catch: You can only claim ITCs if you are registered for HST. This is the primary incentive for businesses earning less than $30,000 to register voluntarily. If you're paying HST on many supplies but not collecting it from customers, you're missing out on this refund.

Filing Your HST Return: Key Considerations

Once registered, you will be assigned a reporting period by the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA)—most commonly annually for small businesses. When it's time to file, you will calculate:

  1. The total HST you collected from your customers.
  2. The total HST you paid on your business expenses (your ITCs).

You then subtract your ITCs from the HST you collected. If the amount collected is more than the ITCs paid, you remit the difference to the CRA. If you paid more in HST than you collected, you will receive a refund.

Accurate bookkeeping is non-negotiable for this process. Diligently tracking your income and every expense with HST is the only way to ensure you are filing correctly and maximizing your eligible ITCs.

Conclusion

Navigating HST is a fundamental aspect of running a successful business in Durham Region. By understanding the registration threshold, leveraging Input Tax Credits, and maintaining meticulous records, you can manage your obligations effectively.
