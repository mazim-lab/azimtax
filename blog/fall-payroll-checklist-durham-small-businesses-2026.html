Fall Payroll Checklist for Durham Region Small Businesses: 7 Things to Review in September 2026

September is a smart time for Durham Region business owners to review payroll before the final stretch of the year. Summer staffing changes, vacation pay, fluctuating hours, and rushed remittances can create small errors that turn into expensive problems later. A short payroll cleanup now can reduce stress, improve records, and help you avoid CRA penalties.

If you run a small business in Oshawa, Whitby, Ajax, Pickering, or Clarington, payroll is not just about paying people on time. It also affects CRA compliance, bookkeeping accuracy, and year-end reporting. Here are seven payroll items worth checking in September 2026.

1. Confirm every payroll remittance has been sent on time

Start with the most important item: your source deduction remittances. Review whether CPP, EI, and income tax withholdings were remitted to the CRA by the correct due dates. If your payroll schedule changed during the summer or if you hired temporary staff, this is where mistakes often show up. Even when the payroll amounts are right, late remittances can still trigger penalties and interest. It is much better to catch a missed or delayed payment in September than during a year-end review.

2. Match payroll reports to what actually cleared the bank

Your payroll system should agree with your bank records. Compare employee net pay, CRA remittances, and any benefit payments against the transactions that actually left your account. This helps catch duplicate runs, missed direct deposits, or manual adjustments that were never entered properly. For owner-managed businesses, it also helps separate real payroll from shareholder draws or ad hoc transfers that should not be treated as wages.

3. Review vacation pay balances before fall schedules get busy

Vacation pay is easy to mishandle when staffing changes or hours increase during the summer. Check whether vacation pay has been accrued properly, whether any payouts were recorded correctly, and whether employees still have outstanding balances that should be tracked. This is especially useful for businesses with part-time, seasonal, or hourly workers. Clean vacation pay records now make future payroll runs easier and reduce disputes later.

4. Check employee records for address, SIN, and tax form updates

Payroll compliance depends on clean employee files, not only clean math. Review whether new hires have complete onboarding records, whether addresses are current, and whether TD1 information is up to date where needed. Missing or outdated employee data can create headaches when preparing T4 slips and responding to CRA questions. September is a good checkpoint because there is still time to fix documentation gaps well before year-end.

5. Verify taxable benefits and allowances are being handled correctly

Vehicle allowances, personal use of company assets, gift cards, and certain reimbursements may have payroll implications. Small businesses sometimes let these items drift outside the payroll process until year-end, which creates messy adjustments later. Review any non-standard compensation provided so far in 2026 and confirm whether it should be treated as a taxable benefit. If the treatment is unclear, it is better to clarify it now than to rebuild payroll history after December.

6. Make sure payroll accounts in the books still make sense

Payroll problems are often bookkeeping problems in disguise. Wages, employer deductions, vacation pay, and remittance liabilities should be posting to the right accounts. If payroll is recorded to generic expense buckets or if liability balances are not clearing after remittances, your financial statements may be misleading. A September review lets you fix coding issues while the trail is still easy to follow. It also makes month-end reporting and cash flow planning more reliable.

7. Get ahead of year-end instead of waiting for December

The best payroll systems do not scramble at the end of the year. Use September to list what will need attention before T4 season: benefit adjustments, bonus planning, owner compensation decisions, and any catch-up entries for prior months. If your business expects stronger sales in the fall, payroll costs may rise quickly. Planning now helps you budget for remittances and avoid surprises when the calendar gets crowded.

Why a September payroll review matters

Payroll errors usually start small. A missed remittance, an incorrect vacation entry, or a taxable benefit that was ignored for a few months does not seem dramatic at first. But by year-end, those issues can become time-consuming and expensive. A September payroll checklist gives small business owners a practical reset point. It improves compliance, strengthens bookkeeping, and makes year-end filing far less painful.
