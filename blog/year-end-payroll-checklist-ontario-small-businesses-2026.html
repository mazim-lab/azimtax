Year-End Payroll Checklist for Ontario Small Businesses: 8 Steps Before Your Final 2026 Pay Run

Year-end payroll problems usually begin with small errors: an old employee address, a benefit that was never entered, or a remittance posted to the wrong account. For employers in Oshawa, Whitby, Ajax, Pickering, Clarington, and across Durham Region, late October is a good time to review payroll before holiday schedules and the final pay runs of 2026. This checklist covers the records and calculations worth checking now.

1. Confirm employee information

Ask employees to review their legal name, mailing address, Social Insurance Number, and province of employment. T4 slips use this information, and corrections are easier before slips are prepared. Check each employee's start date and, where applicable, termination date. If someone left during the year, confirm that the Record of Employment was filed and that the payroll account no longer treats the person as active.

2. Reconcile payroll to your general ledger

Compare year-to-date payroll reports with the wage, vacation pay, bonus, benefit, payroll tax, and remittance accounts in your books. Gross wages in the payroll system should agree with the amount recorded in the general ledger. Review any differences instead of posting one large adjustment without support. Common causes include manual cheques, voided payments, reimbursements coded as wages, or payroll entries recorded twice.

3. Review CPP, EI, and income tax deductions

Check year-to-date employee deductions and employer contributions against your payroll register. Employees who changed payroll systems, worked for a related company, or reached annual contribution limits can require closer review. Do not refund or change statutory deductions based on a guess. Investigate the employee's history and the applicable CRA rules first, then document any correction made through payroll.

4. Record taxable benefits before the last pay run

Review benefits provided during 2026, including employer-paid parking, personal use of a company vehicle, certain gifts or awards, group insurance amounts, housing, and low-interest loans. The tax treatment depends on the benefit and the circumstances. Some amounts affect income tax, CPP, or EI differently. Give your payroll provider or bookkeeper the supporting calculations early enough to include taxable benefits in year-end reporting.

5. Check vacation pay, bonuses, and commissions

Review accrued vacation balances and the method used to calculate vacation pay. Ontario employment standards set minimum requirements, but an employment agreement or company policy may provide more. Confirm the timing and approval of any year-end bonus or commission before entering it. The payment date can affect the employee's 2026 income and the employer's remittance obligation, so avoid backdating payroll entries.

6. Match CRA remittances to payroll reports

Prepare a month-by-month or pay-period reconciliation of income tax, CPP, and EI withheld, employer contributions, payments made to the CRA, and any balance outstanding. Your remittance deadline depends on the CRA remitter category assigned to the business. If a payment appears missing or was applied to the wrong period, investigate it before the next deadline. Keep confirmation numbers and statements with the payroll records.

7. Prepare for T4 slips and the T4 Summary

T4 slips and the related summary are generally due by the last day of February following the calendar year. Because February 28, 2027 falls on a Sunday, the CRA's next-business-day rule would generally make Monday, March 1, 2027 the filing deadline. Do not wait until then. Reconcile each T4 box to payroll records, confirm the totals on the summary, and provide employees with their slips once the information has been checked.

8. Protect and retain payroll records

Store payroll registers, timesheets, employment agreements, TD1 forms, benefit calculations, remittance confirmations, and T4 working papers in an organized folder. Access should be limited because payroll files contain sensitive personal information. CRA generally requires payroll records to be kept for six years from the end of the last tax year they relate to, although specific circumstances can change the retention period.

A short review now can prevent expensive corrections

Schedule the review before your final regular payroll of the year. Start with employee data, reconcile the books, and then work through benefits, remittances, and T4 preparation. If the numbers do not agree, keep a record of what was investigated and how it was corrected. Clean payroll records make year-end filings faster and give employees accurate slips the first time.
