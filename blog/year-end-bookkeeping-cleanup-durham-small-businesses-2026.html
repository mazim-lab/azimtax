Year-End Bookkeeping Cleanup for Durham Region Small Businesses: 8 Tasks to Finish Before December 31

Year-end bookkeeping is easier when it starts before the holiday rush. For small businesses in Oshawa, Whitby, Ajax, Pickering, and across Durham Region, an October or November cleanup can uncover missing receipts, unreconciled transactions, and payroll issues while there is still time to fix them. These eight practical tasks will help you build cleaner 2026 records and prepare for HST, payroll, and income tax filings.

1. Reconcile every bank and credit card account

Match each account in your bookkeeping software to the corresponding bank or credit card statement. Investigate old outstanding cheques, duplicate entries, bank fees, transfers recorded as income, and payments posted to the wrong account. Reconciliation confirms that the books reflect actual cash activity. Complete it through the latest available statement now, then schedule a final reconciliation after December 31.

2. Collect missing receipts and supplier invoices

Review transactions that have no supporting document attached. Ask employees and contractors for missing receipts, download invoices from online accounts, and save emailed bills in a consistent digital folder. A bank statement proves that money moved, but it may not explain the business purpose or provide the details needed to support an HST input tax credit. Record the supplier, date, amount, tax, and reason for each purchase.

3. Review accounts receivable

Run an aged accounts receivable report and follow up on overdue customer invoices before year-end. Confirm that paid invoices are not still shown as outstanding and that customer deposits have been applied correctly. Flag genuinely uncollectible balances for discussion with your tax preparer rather than deleting them from the books. A current receivables list improves cash-flow planning and prevents revenue from being counted twice.

4. Check accounts payable and recurring expenses

Review unpaid supplier bills and recurring subscriptions. Remove duplicates, confirm due dates, and identify services that are no longer used. If your business uses accrual accounting, costs incurred in 2026 may need to be recorded even when payment occurs in 2027. Keep invoices for professional fees, utilities, insurance, advertising, software, and other year-end expenses so they can be assigned to the proper period.

5. Separate business and personal transactions

Owner-paid expenses and personal purchases made from a business account are common cleanup problems. Identify them now and post them consistently to the appropriate shareholder, owner contribution, or draw account. Do not leave personal transactions mixed into operating expenses. Clear separation makes financial reports more useful and reduces the risk of unsupported deductions appearing on a tax return.

6. Review HST collected and input tax credits

Compare HST collected on sales with your invoices and confirm that eligible input tax credits are supported by proper documents. Watch for expenses where tax was coded incorrectly, including zero-rated purchases, exempt services, meals and entertainment, vehicle costs, and purchases from suppliers that did not charge HST. Also verify that prior HST payments or refunds were posted to the HST account rather than income or expenses.

7. Confirm payroll records and contractor payments

Reconcile gross wages, income tax, CPP, EI, employer contributions, benefits, and CRA remittances to your payroll reports. Check employee names, addresses, and Social Insurance Numbers before T4 preparation begins. Review payments to subcontractors separately and confirm that workers have been classified appropriately. Construction businesses should also identify amounts that may need to be reported on a T5018 information return.

8. Build a year-end package for your accountant

Create one organized folder containing December statements, loan documents, major purchase invoices, vehicle information, payroll summaries, HST returns, and details of unusual transactions. Include a list of equipment bought or sold and any financing agreements signed during the year. Add short notes where the business purpose is not obvious. A complete package reduces follow-up questions and helps your accountant finish the year efficiently.

Make year-end a process, not a scramble

Set a weekly cleanup appointment from now through December. Start with reconciliations, then resolve missing documents and review the reports that affect cash flow and tax filings. Clean books will not eliminate every year-end adjustment, but they give you better numbers for pricing, spending, and tax-planning decisions before 2026 closes.
