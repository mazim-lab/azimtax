CRA Deadlines for Small Businesses in Ontario: What to Watch After Tax Season

Tax season may be over, but CRA compliance does not slow down. For Ontario small businesses, the weeks after personal return season are a good time to reset your filing calendar, clean up records, and make sure the next deadline does not catch you off guard.

Why deadlines matter even when business is busy

Most CRA penalties do not start with a major mistake. They start with something small, like a late remittance, a missed HST filing, or a return that was prepared from incomplete books. Once that happens, the problem can snowball into interest, stress, and extra time spent fixing records later.

That is why the best approach is simple: know your filing rhythm, keep cash set aside, and review obligations before they become urgent.

1. HST filing and payment deadlines

If your business is registered for HST, your filing frequency matters. Some businesses file quarterly, others annually, and some have monthly reporting. The deadline is usually tied to your reporting period, not when you happen to have time.

  • Confirm your filing frequency with the CRA.
  • Reconcile sales and input tax credits before filing.
  • Set aside collected HST in a separate account so the payment is ready.

A common mistake is treating HST as extra revenue. It is not. It is money collected on behalf of the government, so the cash should be preserved for remittance.

2. Payroll remittances need consistency

If you have employees, payroll deadlines come with their own rhythm. Source deductions, CPP, EI, and employer remittances need to be sent on time. Missing even one remittance can create interest and compliance issues, especially if payroll is being run manually or through a system that is not reconciled regularly.

To stay ahead:

  1. Match each payroll run to the remittance schedule.
  2. Separate payroll cash from operating cash as soon as wages are paid.
  3. Review year-to-date payroll reports monthly, not once a year.

3. Instalments for income tax or corporate tax

Many owners forget that instalments can become part of the schedule once business income rises. This applies to both individuals and corporations. If income has improved this year, your instalments may need to increase too.

A simple check is to compare current-year profit with last year’s final tax bill. If your profit is trending upward, it may be time to reserve more cash each month rather than waiting for a surprise balance due at year-end.

4. Corporate filing deadlines are easy to miss

Incorporated businesses have a different calendar from sole proprietors. Corporate tax returns, annual resolutions, dividend records, and director updates all need attention. The return itself is only part of the picture. Poor recordkeeping around shareholder loans or owner withdrawals can create extra tax work later.

If your corporation is active, spring is a good time to confirm:

  • Year-end working papers are complete.
  • Shareholder transactions are recorded properly.
  • Financial statements agree with the tax return.

5. Recordkeeping is the deadline before the deadline

Good books make every CRA filing easier. Bad books turn every deadline into a scramble. That is why the smartest deadline habit is actually bookkeeping discipline.

Keep these habits in place:

  • Capture receipts as soon as they are received.
  • Reconcile bank and credit card accounts every month.
  • Use clear categories for meals, mileage, software, and advertising.
  • Keep personal and business spending separate.

If your records are behind, do not wait for the next filing date to clean them up. Fixing the books early is almost always cheaper than fixing them under pressure.

6. CRA notices deserve same-week attention

Some of the most expensive mistakes happen when a CRA notice sits unopened. Whether it is a review letter, a balance notice, or a request for information, the right move is to respond quickly and document everything.

Even if you do not have all the answers right away, acknowledging the notice and getting organized is better than letting the deadline pass silently.

A simple post-tax-season checklist

If you want a practical reset after filing season, work through this short list:

  • Confirm your next HST due date.
  • Review payroll remittance timing.
  • Check whether instalments are due this year.
  • Clean up unreconciled transactions.
  • File or archive all CRA notices.
