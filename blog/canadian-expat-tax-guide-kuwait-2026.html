Canadian Expat Tax Guide for Kuwait: 7 Steps Before You Move in 2026

A move from Canada to Kuwait can bring a new job, a different lifestyle, and a long list of practical decisions. Canadian tax obligations should be near the top of that list. Your tax status does not change simply because you receive a Kuwaiti work permit or spend most of the year abroad. If you are leaving Durham Region or elsewhere in Canada for Kuwait in 2026, these seven steps can help you prepare a cleaner departure and avoid surprises from the Canada Revenue Agency.

1. Determine whether you will become a non-resident of Canada

The CRA considers all the facts when deciding whether someone has ceased Canadian tax residency. Important residential ties may include a home available to you in Canada, a spouse or dependants who remain here, provincial health coverage, personal property, financial accounts, and the regularity of your return visits. No single checklist decides every case. Document your expected departure date, living arrangements in Kuwait, employment contract, and the Canadian ties you plan to keep or sever.

2. Plan the tax return for your year of departure

If you become a non-resident, your Canadian return for the year of departure will generally report worldwide income up to your departure date and certain Canadian-source income afterward. You may also need to disclose your departure date and address. Because employment income, bonuses, investment sales, and moving dates can overlap, collect your T4 slips, final pay statements, travel records, and foreign employment documents before filing season begins.

3. Review whether departure tax applies to your assets

Canada may treat certain property as though it were sold at fair market value when you emigrate, even if you continue to own it. This deemed disposition can apply to some non-registered investments, shares, and other capital property, while important exceptions may apply to assets such as Canadian real estate and registered plans. Create an asset list with cost amounts and market values as of your departure date. Professional advice is especially useful if you own a private corporation, a large portfolio, or property with substantial unrealized gains.

4. Check your TFSA, RRSP, and investment accounts

You can generally keep an existing TFSA after leaving Canada, but contributions made while you are a non-resident can attract a monthly penalty, and you do not build new TFSA contribution room for full non-resident years. RRSPs can often remain open, although future withdrawals may be subject to Canadian withholding tax. Ask each financial institution whether it will continue serving Kuwait residents and update your residency and mailing details promptly.

5. Set up Canadian rental property correctly

If you rent out a home or condo after moving to Kuwait, non-resident withholding rules can apply to the gross rent. A Canadian agent may need to remit tax to the CRA, and an approved NR6 process may allow withholding on estimated net rent instead. A Section 216 return can sometimes reconcile the withholding with actual income and expenses. Arrange property management, receipts, repairs, and monthly statements before departure so your Canadian rental bookkeeping remains complete.

6. Track Canadian income after your move

Becoming a non-resident does not automatically end Canadian tax obligations. Canadian employment income, rental income, pensions, dividends, and gains from Canadian real estate may remain subject to Canadian tax or withholding. Keep tax slips and note whether the payer has your correct non-resident status. Kuwait's treatment of income does not determine your Canadian tax residency, so do not rely only on local payroll practices when assessing your CRA obligations.

7. Build a permanent departure file

Keep copies of your passport travel history, Kuwaiti visa or residency documents, employment contract, lease, utility records, Canadian property documents, account updates, and professional advice. Retain valuations for assets that may be affected by departure tax. A well-organized digital file supports the position taken on your return and makes it easier to answer a future CRA question without reconstructing events years later.

Plan before the move, not after the deadline

The best time to review Canadian expat tax issues is before leaving. Residency, departure tax, investment accounts, and rental withholding can each require action on a different timeline. A pre-departure review gives you time to gather valuations, notify financial institutions, organize Canadian-source income, and understand what must still be filed from Kuwait.
