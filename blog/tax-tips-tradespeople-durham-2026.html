Key Tax Tips for Tradespeople and Construction Businesses in Durham Region

Published on March 4, 2026

The skilled trades are the backbone of Durham Region's growth, from new housing developments in Whitby to commercial projects in Oshawa. As a contractor, electrician, plumber, or carpenter, you're an expert in your craft. However, navigating the complexities of the Canadian tax system can be just as challenging as any job site. Proper tax planning and bookkeeping are crucial for maximizing your earnings and ensuring long-term financial health.

At Azim Tax & Accounting, we work with many tradespeople across the region. Here are our top tax tips to help you build a solid financial foundation.

1. Master Your Vehicle Expense Claims

For most tradespeople, your truck or van is your mobile office. The costs associated with it are one of your most significant business deductions. To claim these expenses correctly, you must:

  • Keep a detailed logbook: This is non-negotiable. The CRA requires a log of all your business mileage, including the date, destination, purpose, and kilometres driven. A simple notebook or a GPS-tracking app can save you thousands of dollars in the event of an audit.
  • Track all vehicle costs: This includes fuel, insurance, registration, maintenance, repairs, loan interest, and lease payments.
  • Calculate the business-use percentage: At year-end, divide your total business kilometres by the total kilometres driven to get your business-use percentage. You can then claim this percentage of your total vehicle costs.

2. Understand the Tools and Equipment Deduction

The cost of tools and equipment is another major deduction. The rules vary depending on whether you are an employee or self-employed:

  • Self-Employed Contractors: You can deduct the full cost of small tools under $500 in the year of purchase. More expensive equipment is considered a capital asset and must be depreciated over several years through the Capital Cost Allowance (CCA) system.
  • Employed Tradespeople: You may be able to deduct the cost of tools you are required to purchase for your job. To do so, your employer must complete and sign CRA Form T2200, Declaration of Conditions of Employment.

3. Navigate HST/GST Correctly

If your business earns over $30,000 in revenue in any four consecutive quarters, you are required to register for, collect, and remit HST. This is a common area of confusion.

  • Collecting HST: You must charge HST on all your invoices for labour and materials.
  • Input Tax Credits (ITCs): The HST you pay on your business expenses (materials, tools, fuel, etc.) can be claimed back as ITCs. This reduces the net amount of tax you owe to the government.
  • Filing Frequency: Depending on your revenue, you will file HST annually, quarterly, or monthly. Keeping your bookkeeping up-to-date is essential to avoid missing these deadlines.

4. Subcontractors vs. Employees: Know the Difference

Many construction businesses hire subcontractors to help with projects. It is critical to correctly classify these workers. The CRA looks at factors like control over the work, ownership of tools, and financial risk to determine status.

Misclassifying an employee as a subcontractor can lead to significant financial penalties, including being forced to pay back payroll deductions (CPP and EI) for that worker, plus penalties and interest. When in doubt, it's always best to seek professional advice.

Conclusion

As a skilled tradesperson in Durham Region, your focus should be on your clients and your craft. By implementing these tax tips and maintaining organized records, you can ensure you are not overpaying on your taxes and are protected from CRA scrutiny.
