5 Common Bookkeeping Mistakes Durham Businesses Make (And How to Fix Them)

Published on February 27, 2026

Clean, accurate books are the backbone of any successful business in Durham Region. They provide the financial clarity needed for smart decision-making and are essential for staying on the right side of the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). Yet, many small business owners fall into common bookkeeping traps that can cost them time, money, and a lot of stress.

At Azim Tax & Accounting, we've helped countless local businesses from Oshawa to Pickering clean up their books. Here are five of the most common mistakes we see and how you can avoid them.

1. Mixing Personal and Business Finances

This is the number one mistake. Paying for a business expense with a personal credit card or vice-versa creates a nightmare for tracking and tax filing. It makes it difficult to see how your business is actually performing and can lead the CRA to disallow legitimate business expenses during an audit.

The Fix: From day one, open a dedicated business bank account and get a business credit card. Funnel all business income and expenses through these accounts exclusively. It’s the simplest and most effective way to maintain a clear financial record.

2. Not Tracking Small Cash Expenses

A $5 coffee with a client or a $10 parking fee might seem insignificant, but these small cash outlays add up over a year. Failing to track them means you're leaving money on the table and overpaying on your taxes.

The Fix: Use a dedicated app like QuickBooks Online to snap photos of receipts the moment you get them. If you prefer a manual system, have a designated envelope in your vehicle or wallet for all cash receipts and log them into your bookkeeping software weekly.

3. Inconsistent Categorization of Expenses

One month, you might categorize your Adobe software subscription as "Software," the next as "Office Supplies," and the third as "Advertising." This inconsistency makes your financial reports unreliable and prevents you from accurately tracking where your money is going.

The Fix: Create a simple Chart of Accounts that lists all your expense categories. Be consistent. A professional bookkeeper can set this up for you, ensuring your spending is categorized correctly for both management and tax purposes every single time.

4. Not Reconciling Your Books Regularly

Waiting until year-end to reconcile your bank accounts with your books is a recipe for disaster. It can lead to missed transactions, overlooked bank errors, and a poor understanding of your cash flow. You can't make informed business decisions based on outdated, inaccurate data.

The Fix: Make bank reconciliation a monthly habit. This process involves matching every transaction in your bookkeeping software to your bank and credit card statements. Doing it monthly catches errors early and ensures your financial reports are always accurate and up-to-date.

5. Ignoring Payroll Tax Obligations

Hiring your first employee is a major milestone, but it comes with serious responsibilities. Failing to remit payroll deductions (like CPP and EI) to the CRA on time can result in severe penalties and interest that can cripple a small business.

The Fix: Understand your remitter type and schedule from the CRA. Use payroll software that automates these calculations and reminds you of deadlines. Better yet, outsource your payroll to a professional who can manage remittances and ensure you stay compliant, leaving you free to manage your team.
