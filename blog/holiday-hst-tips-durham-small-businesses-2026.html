Holiday HST Tips for Durham Region Small Businesses: 7 Sales and Recordkeeping Checks

Holiday promotions can create a busy mix of gift cards, discounts, online orders, returns, staff purchases, and year-end expenses. For HST-registered businesses in Oshawa, Whitby, Ajax, Pickering, Clarington, and across Durham Region, the challenge is not only making sales—it is recording each transaction correctly. These seven practical HST checks can help retailers, service providers, restaurants, and other small businesses keep cleaner records through the 2026 holiday season.

1. Treat gift card sales and redemptions separately

When a customer buys a gift card or gift certificate, GST/HST is generally not charged on the card's face value. The tax is generally calculated when the card is redeemed for a taxable product or service. Your point-of-sale and bookkeeping systems should therefore distinguish gift card liabilities from ordinary sales revenue. Reconcile cards sold, cards redeemed, and the outstanding balance so you do not report tax too early—or miss it when the customer makes the purchase.

2. Calculate HST after eligible discounts

A store discount offered at the time of sale usually reduces the price on which HST is calculated. The receipt should clearly show the original price, discount, net selling price, and tax. Manufacturer reimbursements, coupons, loyalty rewards, and promotional certificates can have different rules, so avoid assuming every promotion receives identical treatment. Configure each discount type in your sales system and review a sample receipt before a campaign goes live.

3. Check the place-of-supply rules for online orders

Shipping outside Ontario can change the applicable GST/HST rate. The correct rate may depend on the type of supply and where the product is delivered or the service is considered supplied. Capture the customer's delivery details and retain shipping evidence rather than applying Ontario's 13% HST to every online transaction automatically. If you sell digital products, subscriptions, or services across provinces, review your setup with a tax professional because the rules can be more complex.

4. Record returns, exchanges, and store credits properly

Post-holiday returns should reverse the sales amount and related HST when a refund or credit is issued in accordance with the tax rules. Keep the original receipt, return record, date, amount refunded, tax adjustment, and payment method. Exchanges should not be recorded as an undocumented cash difference. A clear audit trail helps your bookkeeper reconcile sales reports to bank deposits and supports adjustments claimed on the HST return.

5. Support input tax credits on holiday expenses

HST paid on eligible business purchases may qualify for an input tax credit, but only when the expense is connected to commercial activities and the required documentation is available. Save supplier invoices for advertising, packaging, delivery, software, temporary equipment, and other seasonal costs. The invoice should identify the supplier, date, amount, and applicable tax, with additional details required for larger purchases. A card statement alone may not show enough information to support the claim.

6. Separate meals, gifts, and personal purchases

Holiday meals, client gifts, staff events, and owner purchases should not all be coded to one general expense account. Income tax limitations and HST input tax credit restrictions may apply, particularly to meals and entertainment. Personal purchases are not business input tax credits simply because they were paid with a company card. Record who attended or received the item, the business purpose, and the nature of the purchase so each transaction can be reviewed correctly.

7. Reconcile sales reports before filing

At month-end or quarter-end, compare point-of-sale totals, e-commerce reports, payment processor statements, bank deposits, and bookkeeping revenue. Differences can arise from tips, delivery fees, gift cards, returns, processing fees, deposits in transit, and payouts that combine several days of sales. Reconcile gross sales and HST before recording processor fees. If the net bank deposit is posted as revenue, both sales and tax can be understated.

Build a holiday HST review into your routine

Set aside time each week to review unusual transactions and clear uncategorized items. Confirm that taxable, zero-rated, and exempt supplies are mapped correctly, and keep source documents in one organized digital folder. Before filing, compare the HST collected and input tax credits with prior periods and investigate unexpected changes. A short review now can prevent a difficult cleanup after the holiday rush.
