Your 2025 Tax Filing Checklist: A Guide to Getting Your Documents in Order

Published on March 7, 2026

As tax season in Durham Region intensifies, the key to a smooth and stress-free filing experience is preparation. Rushing at the last minute often leads to missed deductions and costly errors. Whether you're an employee in Pickering, a freelancer in Whitby, or a small business owner in Oshawa, having your documents organized is the most critical first step. This checklist will help you gather everything you need to file your 2025 tax return accurately.

1. Personal Information

Let's start with the basics. Ensure you have these details handy for yourself, your spouse or common-law partner, and any dependents.

  • Social Insurance Number (SIN)
  • Date of Birth
  • Mailing Address
  • CRA My Account login information (highly recommended for accessing past returns and notices of assessment)
  • Direct deposit information for your refund

2. Income Slips

This is the proof of all the income you earned throughout the year. Most of these slips should be sent to you by the end of February.

  • T4, Statement of Remuneration Paid: If you are an employee, this is the most important slip, showing your employment income and deductions like CPP, EI, and income tax.
  • T4A, Statement of Pension, Retirement, Annuity, and Other Income: For self-employed income, pension payments, or bursaries.
  • T5, Statement of Investment Income: Details income from interest, dividends, and certain foreign sources.
  • T3, Statement of Trust Income Allocations and Designations: For income from mutual funds held in non-registered accounts.
  • T4E, Statement of Employment Insurance and Other Benefits: If you received EI benefits.
  • T5007, Statement of Benefits: For workers' compensation or social assistance payments.
  • T2202, Tuition and Enrolment Certificate: For post-secondary students.

3. Deduction and Credit Receipts

This is where good record-keeping throughout the year pays off. These receipts reduce your taxable income, lowering the amount of tax you owe.

Common for Everyone:

  • RRSP Contribution Receipts: For contributions made in 2025 and the first 60 days of 2026.
  • Medical Expense Receipts: Prescriptions, dental visits, vision care, physiotherapy, and other eligible expenses not covered by insurance.
  • Charitable Donation Receipts: Official receipts from registered charities.
  • Child Care Expense Receipts: Payments made to daycare centres, nannies, or other childcare providers.
  • Political Contribution Receipts: For donations to federal or provincial political parties.

For Small Business Owners & Freelancers:

This is where things get more detailed. You'll need records for all your business expenses, including:

  • Vehicle Expenses: A detailed mileage log (total km driven vs. business km), plus receipts for fuel, insurance, maintenance, and lease payments.
  • Home Office Expenses: A calculation of your workspace's square footage, plus records for utilities, home insurance, property taxes, and rent/mortgage interest.
  • Office Supplies & Expenses: Receipts for stationery, software, phone bills, and internet.
  • Meals and Entertainment: Records of who you met with and for what business purpose (generally 50% deductible).
  • Professional Fees: Invoices for legal, accounting, or consulting services.
  • Bank Statements and Capital Asset Receipts: To track income and document purchases of equipment or machinery.

Don't Forget Your Notice of Assessment (NOA)

Your previous year's Notice of Assessment is a valuable document. It contains your RRSP deduction limit, any unused capital losses, and other amounts that can be carried forward. You can easily access this through your CRA My Account.

Ready to File?

Once you have your documents organized, the filing process becomes much simpler. You can confidently file on your own, or hand everything over to a professional knowing you haven't missed anything.
