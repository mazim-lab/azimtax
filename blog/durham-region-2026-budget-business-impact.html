Durham Region's 2026 Budget Approved: What It Means for Local Business Owners

Published on February 23, 2026

The Durham Regional Council has officially approved the 2026 Business Plans and Budget, confirming a property tax increase of 4.8 per cent. While headlines often focus on the impact for homeowners, this decision has significant ripple effects for the small business community in Oshawa, Whitby, Ajax, Pickering, and Clarington.

As a business owner, understanding municipal budgets is crucial for forecasting your overhead. Here is a breakdown of what the 2026 budget means for your bottom line and the local economy.

The 4.8% Increase: Not Just for Homeowners

The approved 4.8% increase is designed to balance affordability with the need for essential services. For the average homeowner, this translates to about $14 more per month. However, for commercial properties, the calculation is different—and often more substantial.

Impact on Commercial Tenants

If you lease your business space, you might think property taxes are the landlord's problem. In most commercial leases, especially "Triple Net" (NNN) leases, property taxes are flowed through to the tenant. You likely pay a portion of the building's taxes as part of your "TMI" (Taxes, Maintenance, and Insurance) or "Additional Rent."

With a 4.8% regional hike (plus potential local municipal increases), you should expect your TMI payments to rise this year. It is a good time to review your lease agreement and prepare your cash flow for a slight increase in monthly occupancy costs.

Where is the Money Going?

The budget isn't just about taking money; it is about investing in the region's infrastructure, which supports local commerce. Key investments include:

  • Police Services: Approximately $10 of the monthly residential increase is allocated here. A safe community is vital for retail and service businesses.
  • Transit and Roads: Investments in Durham Region Transit (DRT) and regional roads help your employees get to work and ensure goods can move efficiently across the GTA.
  • Housing and Paramedic Services: Essential for a growing population and workforce.

The "Core Services Review"

One interesting note from the budget approval is the region's plan to conduct a Core Services Review. This review will assess how services are delivered and identify "sustainable efficiencies."

For businesses, this is worth watching. It could lead to streamlined processes for permits and inspections, or it could signal future changes in user fees for water, sewer, and waste disposal. The goal is to ensure regional funding doesn't exceed mandates, which hopefully keeps future tax hikes manageable.

Action Items for Business Owners

So, what should you do now?

  1. Adjust Your Budget: If you own your building, factor the 4.8% increase into your 2026 expense projections. If you rent, anticipate a reconciliation statement from your landlord later in the year asking for the difference.
  2. Check Your Assessment: Property taxes are based on the assessed value of your property, set by MPAC (Municipal Property Assessment Corporation). If you believe your commercial property's valuation is incorrect, you can file a Request for Reconsideration.
  3. Focus on Efficiency: With fixed costs like taxes rising, look for internal efficiencies to maintain your margins. This might mean better inventory management, revisiting supplier contracts, or automating your bookkeeping to save time and fees.
